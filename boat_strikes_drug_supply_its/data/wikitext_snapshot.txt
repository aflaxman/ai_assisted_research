The United States military began executing [[airstrikes]] on vessels in the [[Caribbean Sea]] in September 2025, described by the [[Second presidency of Donald Trump|administration of President Donald Trump]] as part of an effort to fight the [[maritime drug trafficking in Latin America|flow of illicit drugs from Latin America]] to the US. In October, the strikes expanded to include vessels in the Eastern [[Pacific Ocean]]. The Trump administration has alleged, without producing public evidence, that the vessels were operated by groups it designated as [[Narcoterrorism|narcoterrorists]], including the Venezuelan terrorist organization [[Tren de Aragua]] and the Colombian [[far-left]] [[Guerrilla warfare|guerilla]] group [[National Liberation Army (Colombia)|National Liberation Army]]. 

As part of what was later unveiled as [[Operation Southern Spear]], the US began [[United States military buildup in the Caribbean during Operation Southern Spear|deploying Navy warships and personnel to the Caribbean]] in mid-August. Trump announced on 2 September that the [[United States Navy|US Navy]] had carried out the first airstrike in the Caribbean on a boat from Venezuela, killing all 11 people aboard; he released a video of the incident, which Venezuelan sources said had occurred on 1 September. US officials said that this was a military operation against drug cartels in Latin America and that these military operations would continue. As of 25 August 2026, at least 227 people had been killed (including 17 who are missing and presumed dead) in at least 68 strikes on 69 vessels and the US had made its [[#Land targets in Venezuela|first strike on a land target within Venezuela]].<ref name=NYTTracking/> On 12 June, [[Niño Guerrero]], leader of Tren de Aragua, was killed in joint operation with the post-Maduro Venezuelan government.<ref name=":4" /> In addition, on 6 March 2026, as part of the [[Ecuadorian conflict (2024–present)|Ecuador conflict]], the United States and [[Ecuadorian Armed Forces]] airstriked a single target allegedly belonging to [[FARC dissidents]].  Starting in 2026, the [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]] conducted a parallel [[covert operation|covert]] strike campaign targeting Ecuadorian fishing vessels near the [[Galápagos Islands]], both in Ecuador's [[exclusive economic zone]] and international waters.<ref name="CIAStrikesGalápagos">{{cite web |last1=Piper |first1=Imogen |last2=Nakashima |first2=Ellen |last3=Schmidt |first3=Samantha |last4=Oakford |first4=Samuel |last5=Strobel |first5=Warren |last6=Horton |first6=Alex |last7=Schaffer |first7=Aaron |title=Covert CIA program said to be behind mysterious attacks on Galápagos boats |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/investigations/2026/08/13/covert-cia-program-said-be-behind-mysterious-attacks-galpagos-boats/?pwapi_token=eyJ0eXAiOiJKV1QiLCJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9.eyJyZWFzb24iOiJnaWZ0IiwibmJmIjoxNzg2NTkzNjAwLCJpc3MiOiJzdWJzY3JpcHRpb25zIiwiZXhwIjoxNzg3OTc1OTk5LCJpYXQiOjE3ODY1OTM2MDAsImp0aSI6IjA0YzZhY2QxLTE5MzYtNDU5Ni04YTgyLTk4NGQxN2Y1MTIzZCIsInVybCI6Imh0dHBzOi8vd3d3Lndhc2hpbmd0b25wb3N0LmNvbS9pbnZlc3RpZ2F0aW9ucy8yMDI2LzA4LzEzL2NvdmVydC1jaWEtcHJvZ3JhbS1zYWlkLWJlLWJlaGluZC1teXN0ZXJpb3VzLWF0dGFja3MtZ2FscGFnb3MtYm9hdHMvIn0.fMwRYlZOdI4r95gdY04PmMbiPUdjLjFwEQLIZN73ehM |website=washingtonpost.com |publisher=The Washington Post |access-date=13 August 2026}}</ref>

The strikes came amid tensions between the [[United States–Venezuela relations|United States and Venezuela]] as Trump steadily increased pressure. Venezuela's [[Nicolás Maduro]] repeatedly accused the US of seeking [[United States involvement in regime change#2019–present: Venezuela|regime change]] which some third-party sources agree with. Some experts, human rights groups and international bodies have said the killings are illegal under US and [[international law]], and the Colombian and Venezuelan governments have accused the US of [[Extrajudicial killing|extrajudicial murder]]. In 2025 the [[United States Republican Party|Republican]]-controlled [[United States Senate|US Senate]] twice rejected resolutions that would limit Trump's authority to continue military action against Venezuela or airstrikes against alleged drug vessels. On 3 January 2026, [[2026 United States strikes in Venezuela|Maduro was captured and flown out of the country]] by US forces.

== Airstrikes on vessels ==
[[File:Lethal Kinetic Strike, Sept 2, 2025 (987835).webm|thumb|Unclassified footage of the first airstrike (1 September)]]
As of 25 March 2026, at least 163 people had been killed (including 3 who are missing and presumed dead) in 47 strikes on 48 vessels, including 15 vessels struck in the Caribbean Sea, 31 in the Eastern Pacific, and 2 in an unspecified location.<ref name=NYTTimeline/><ref name=NYTTracking/><ref name=":0" />

In four of the strikes as of 31 December 2025, crew members survived the initial strike.<ref name="NYT31DecCoastGuard">{{cite news|first1=Eric|last1=Schmitt|title=Coast Guard Searches for Survivors After More Boat Strikes|date=31 December 2025|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2025/12/31/us/politics/coast-guard-survivors-boat-strikes.html|work=The New York Times|access-date=1 January 2026|archive-date=1 January 2026|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260101163327/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/12/31/us/politics/coast-guard-survivors-boat-strikes.html|url-status=live}}</ref> The number of people killed includes two survivors from the initial strike who were killed in subsequent strikes<ref name=WAPO28Nov/><ref name=NBC1Dec/> and one missing survivor of the 27 October strike who is presumed dead.<ref name=NYTCastaway/><ref name=CNN6Nov>{{Cite news |url=https://edition.cnn.com/2025/11/06/politics/us-military-caribbean-boat-strike-kills-3 |title=US military strikes another boat in the Caribbean, killing 3 |last=Voytek |first=Clay |publisher=CNN |date=6 November 2025 |access-date=7 November 2025 |archive-date=12 January 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260112100131/https://edition.cnn.com/2025/11/06/politics/us-military-caribbean-boat-strike-kills-3 |url-status=live }}</ref> Two people who survived the 16 October strike were repatriated to their home countries.<ref name=CBS13Nov/><ref name=ABCTimeline/><ref name=ABC29Oct/> The US Coast Guard suspended a search for an unknown number of survivors of the 30 December strike.<ref name=Reuters3Jan2026/>

===Legality of the strikes===
Experts have questioned the legality of the strikes under US and [[international law]].<ref name=Independent3Sep>{{cite news |last1=Woodward |first1=Alex |date=3 September 2025 |title=Trump's Venezuelan boat violated international law, experts warn |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/americas/us-politics/trump-venezuela-drug-boat-attack-video-b2819566.html |access-date=3 September 2025 |work=The Independent |language=en |archive-date=28 November 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251128112118/https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/americas/us-politics/trump-venezuela-drug-boat-attack-video-b2819566.html |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name=CSIS8Sep>{{cite web |last1=Cancian |first1=Mark F. |last2=Park |first2=Chris H. |date=8 September 2025 |title=Going to War with the Cartels: The Military Implications |url=https://www.csis.org/analysis/going-war-cartels-military-implications |access-date=11 September 2025 |publisher=[[Center for Strategic and International Studies]]}}</ref> The [[United States Coast Guard|US Coast Guard]] or other law enforcement agencies had been responsible for US drug interdiction efforts for decades prior to the strikes, and suspects were prosecuted as a criminal matter,<ref name=WAPO28Nov/><ref name=CNN28Nov/> but according to CNN, the DOJ argued before the strikes began "that the president is legally allowed to authorize lethal strikes against 24 cartels and criminal organizations in self-defense, because the groups pose an imminent threat to Americans".<ref name=CNN28Nov/>

Experts speaking to the BBC said that the 2 September strike was potentially illegal under international [[maritime law|maritime]] and [[human rights law]]. Though the US is not a signatory to the [[United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea]], previous US policy had been to "act in a manner consistent with its provisions"; countries are not supposed to interfere with ships in international waters except in cases such as [[hot pursuit]] out of a country's [[territorial waters]].<ref name=BBCVerify>{{cite news |last1=Murphy |first1=Matt |last2=Cheetham |first2=Joshua |date=4 September 2025 |title=US strike on 'Venezuela drug boat': What do we know, and was it legal? |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cdjzw3gplv7o |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260624161421/https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cdjzw3gplv7o |archive-date=24 June 2026 |access-date=5 September 2025 |agency=[[BBC]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=right of hot pursuit |url=https://www.oxfordreference.com/display/10.1093/oi/authority.20110803095946378 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251021103846/https://www.oxfordreference.com/display/10.1093/oi/authority.20110803095946378 |archive-date=21 October 2025 |access-date=5 September 2025 |website=Oxford Reference |quote=The right of a coastal state to pursue a foreign ship within its territorial waters ...&nbsp;and there capture it if the state has good reason to believe that this vessel has violated its laws. Trump's gamble over Venezuela |url=https://www.thetimes.com/us/american-politics/article/war-on-drugs-or-regime-change-trumps-gamble-over-venezuela-gg2fcn5jc |url-access=subscription |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260113051212/https://www.thetimes.com/us/american-politics/article/war-on-drugs-or-regime-change-trumps-gamble-over-venezuela-gg2fcn5jc |archive-date=13 January 2026 |access-date=18 October 2025 |work=[[The Sunday Times]]}}</ref>

In November 2025, [[Pope Leo XIV]] noted that the strikes were gradually nearing Venezuela's coastline and stated that they were only "increasing tension"; he called for the United States and Venezuela to "seek dialogue".<ref>{{cite news |title=Pope Leo calls for 'deep reflection' about treatment of detained migrants in the United States |url=https://apnews.com/article/vatican-pope-migrants-us-441229638a27420cad3de1e07f73a494 |access-date=7 November 2025 |work=Associated Press |date=5 November 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251106142532/https://apnews.com/article/vatican-pope-migrants-us-441229638a27420cad3de1e07f73a494 |archive-date=6 November 2025 |url-status=live}}</ref>

{{See also|#Inter-American Commission on Human Rights}}
The [[Inter-American Commission on Human Rights]] called "upon the United States to: refrain from employing lethal military force in the context of public security operations, ensuring that any counter-crime or security operation fully complies with international human rights standards; conduct prompt, impartial, and independent investigations into all deaths and detentions resulting from these actions; and adopt effective measures to prevent recurrence" and urged the US to "to ensure that all security operations, including those carried out beyond its borders, are consistent with international human rights obligations, particularly regarding the protection of the right to life, the use of force, due process guarantees, and accountability mechanisms."<ref>{{cite web |title=IACHR urges the United States to ensure respect for human rights in extraterritorial security operations |url=https://www.oas.org/en/IACHR/jsForm/?File=/en/iachr/media_center/PReleases/2025/248.asp |website=[[Inter-American Commission on Human Rights]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251203033731/https://www.oas.org/en/IACHR/jsForm/?File=/en/iachr/media_center/PReleases/2025/248.asp |archive-date=December 3, 2025 |language=en |date=December 2, 2025 |url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Opinion polls ===
A [[Center for American Political Studies|Harvard CAPS]]/Harris poll from 1–2 October found that 71% of respondents supported the US destroying boats trafficking drugs from South America.<ref>{{Cite press release|title=September 2025 – Harvard CAPS / Harris Poll |url=https://harvardharrispoll.com/press-release-september-2025/ |access-date=24 October 2025 |language=en-US|date= 6 October 2025}}</ref>

A [[Reuters]]/[[Ipsos]] poll from 7–12 November found that 51% of Americans opposed "killing suspected drug traffickers abroad without judicial process", while 29% supported it.<ref>{{cite news|first1=Jason |last1=Lange |first2=Matt |last2=Spetalnick|url=https://www.reuters.com/world/us/just-29-americans-support-us-military-killing-drug-suspects-reutersipsos-poll-2025-11-14/ |title=Just 29% of Americans support US military killing drug suspects, Reuters/Ipsos poll finds |publisher=Reuters|date= 14 November 2025|access-date=15 November 2025}}</ref>
