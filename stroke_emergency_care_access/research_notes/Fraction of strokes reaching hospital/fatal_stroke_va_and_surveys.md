# Indirect windows on whether strokes reach hospital: place of death and care before death among stroke deaths (verbal autopsy, sample registration, vital registration), and household or ageing surveys of stroke survivors

Retrieved 2026-11-25. Sources were PubMed (abstracts via the PubMed tools and E-utilities), PubMed Central full-text XML (searched for the quoted sentences and table rows), survey questionnaires downloaded as PDF and converted to text, the public WHO verbal autopsy repository, and a small number of web pages read through a fetch tool. Text inside double quotation marks is verbatim from material retrieved in this session; where a table row is quoted, its cells are separated by semicolons. Values marked "derived" are my arithmetic on counts that are quoted alongside. Anything I could not open is listed under Gaps as "Could not verify", with what I tried.

Notation used throughout: A = fraction of all incident strokes admitted to hospital in the acute phase (the target). H = fraction of stroke deaths that occur in a hospital or health facility. C = fraction of stroke deaths with any hospital admission or hospital contact during the final illness. S = fraction of stroke survivors alive at interview who were hospitalised or treated.

## Key question 1. Verbal autopsy and sample registration: what share of stroke deaths occurred at home, and what share had sought hospital care before death? Which VA instruments carry the items needed for a harmonised re-analysis?

### Takeaway
Stroke-specific place of death from verbal autopsy is published for surprisingly few populations: about 87% of stroke deaths at home in rural Gadchiroli (2011-13), 46% at home plus 15% in transit in Faridabad (2019-20, ages 30-69), and 79% at home across China's national mortality surveillance (2008-2020, 3.2 million stroke deaths, flat over time). Where both were measured, hospital contact before death was well above the in-hospital death share (Faridabad 56% versus 38%), and in northern Vietnam 57% of VA stroke deaths had been admitted at some point, so H is a lower bound on C, and the WHO 2022 VA revision dropped the care-seeking items that would let C be computed from new data.

Further cited points:

- Ke et al. (Million Death Study, Lancet Glob Health 2018) do not report place of death or hospital care for stroke deaths in the main text. The paper says only "In India, most deaths occur at home and are not captured in official records", and its Table 1 gives about 23 thousand male and 18 thousand female study stroke deaths for 2001-13, so a national VA sample of roughly 41,000 stroke deaths exists but is untabulated by place. The questionnaire "captures awareness of any previous physician-diagnosed condition such as heart disease and stroke, and in 2004 we modified the questionnaire to include medication use." — [Ke et al. 2018, doi:10.1016/S2214-109X(18)30242-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X(18)30242-0)
- A companion Million Death Study paper states "We had no autopsy or neuroimaging data because most deaths occurred at home." — [Glob Heart 2021, doi:10.5334/gh.1048](https://doi.org/10.5334/gh.1048)
- Stroke deaths are early in rural India: in rural Ludhiana (262 VA stroke deaths, Dec 2016 to Nov 2018) "219 (83.6%) of deaths occurred within 30 days of symptom onset, with 126 (48.1%) occurring within the first seven days". The paper does not report place of death. — [Cureus 2026, doi:10.7759/cureus.103592](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.103592)
- In Faridabad the terminal event was fast: "The median (IQR) time interval between the onset of symptoms and death was 2.0 (0.76, 12.0) hours in a cardiac event and 22.4 (1.8, 75.9) hours in a stroke". The authors note "since only deaths were covered this does not reflect the delays in all acute cardiac or stroke cases" and that VA was not possible "in 11% deaths due to COVID-19 restrictions". — [Krishnan et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lansea.2023.100222)
- In China, home death does not mean no hospital contact. The CKB authors write "Previous studies reported that ≥70% of deaths in China occurred at home with ≥90% of death certificates had medical visit history", and ignoring discharge-to-die-at-home understates in-hospital case fatality by "29·7%, 35·7%, 41·5%, and 48·1%" for 3, 7, 14 and 30-day windows. — [Weng et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanwpc.2021.100301)
- The China DSP background as described by its custodians: "75% of deaths occur at home, and a further 8.6% of deaths lack sufficient medical or other background information to accurately determine the CoD. In addition, 5.5% of deaths are of individuals who had no contact with medical facilities". — [Popul Health Metr 2021, doi:10.1186/s12963-021-00256-1](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12963-021-00256-1)
- Within China the hospital share of CVD deaths ranges from Shanghai (59.40%) and Beijing (50.32%) down to provinces where home deaths exceed 90% (Fujian 93.14%, Hainan 90.97%), and haemorrhagic stroke deaths were less likely than ischaemic stroke deaths to occur in hospital ("HS (OR: 0·75, 95%CI: 0·71-0·79)"). — [Lancet Reg Health West Pac 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanwpc.2022.100383)
- The China NMSS death record also carries "Highest diagnostic institutions" (for all CVD deaths: village clinics 18.34%, primary institutions 42.58%, secondary institutions 26.64%, remainder not extracted), which is a second, unexploited indicator of health-system contact before death. — [same source, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanwpc.2022.100383)
- In Agincourt, care seeking for fatal cardiovascular illness often bypassed hospital: "Half of cases initially sought care outside a hospital setting (50.9%, 199/391)", and the free text records refusal of hospital ("She refused to go to the hospital, she died at home") and discharge while still ill (40 of 151 with a delay). — [BMJ Open 2021](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2020-048592)
- Thai VA study design confirms that out-of-hospital deaths are registered from family report plus "documentary evidence, wherever possible, from previous hospitalization or medical attention during the illness preceding death". — [Rao et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1186/1478-7954-8-11)
- A regional review repeats the Gadchiroli figure as the stock fact for South-East Asia: "in a study from rural India, close to 90% of deaths from stroke occurred at home and thus were not medically certified." — [Lancet Reg Health SE Asia 2023, doi:10.1016/j.lansea.2023.100286](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lansea.2023.100286)

### Inferences
- H (in-facility share of stroke deaths) understates C (hospital contact before a fatal stroke) wherever both exist: Faridabad 38.4% versus 56.2%; in China 17.6% of stroke deaths are in institutions while 35.7% of people hospitalised in their last week go home to die and "≥90% of death certificates had medical visit history". Treat H as a floor for C, not as an estimate of it.
- The ratio C/H is not constant. It is about 1.5 in Faridabad and plausibly 3 or more in China, driven by a culture of dying at home and by insurance scheme. A model that maps H to A needs a region-specific offset (Adair's East Asia coefficient is the same phenomenon in all-cause data), so H is more defensible for within-region gradients and trends than for levels.
- The published VA record is thin for the exact quantity wanted. Most HDSS and national VA papers report cause fractions and all-cause place of death separately. The microdata needed already exist (Id10058 by cause in every WHO-instrument dataset; Id10432, Id10433 and Id10450 in 2012 and 2016-instrument datasets), so a harmonised re-analysis across COMSA Mozambique, HEAL-SL, Zambia SAVVY, Tanzania, INDEPTH sites, CHARLS exit interviews and the Million Death Study is feasible, but it is a data-access project, not a literature extraction.
- Time series exist for few places: China NMSS 2008-2020 (flat), Chongqing 2012-2021 (home share rising from 75% to 79%), and India SRS all-cause "no hospital attention" (72%, 66%, 52% in 2006, 2011, 2018). The Indian series moves in the expected direction and by a large amount; the Chinese series does not move at all over 2008-2020, a period the CKB authors describe as one of "expansion of hospital beds", which is itself a warning that place of death may be a poor tracker of admission in China.
- Stroke deaths identified by VA at home rest on a history of paralysis and are less specific than hospital diagnoses, and registration-based samples (Faridabad, Chongqing, Mahajanga) omit unregistered deaths, which are more often home deaths. Both push published H upward relative to the truth for all stroke deaths.

## Key question 2. Vital registration with place of death: which countries publish cause-specific place of death, and what cross-country compilations exist?

### Takeaway
Cause-specific place of death for cerebrovascular disease is published as long series for Brazil (78% of stroke-mention deaths in hospital, 2000-2019), the United States (about 43 to 47% in hospital, 29 to 35% in nursing homes), Sweden (47% hospital, 44% nursing home), Japan and China, and the microdata fields exist in Brazil, Mexico and other Latin American systems. The cross-country compilations (Adair 2021, Broad 2013, Pivodic 2016) are all-cause or palliative-care groupings; none tabulates stroke, though Adair holds cause-specific data for Chile, Colombia, Mexico and Uruguay.

### Inferences
- A cerebrovascular "in-hospital death share" time series could be built from public microdata for Brazil (2000 onward), Mexico (1990 onward) and the United States (1999 onward), and probably Colombia, Chile and Uruguay through the sources Adair used. That is perhaps eight to twelve countries with long series, almost all upper-middle or high income, with China and Japan available through published tabulations.
- Brazil and Mexico also record whether the decedent received medical care during the fatal illness (ASSISTMED, asist_medi). That item is closer to C than place of death and has not, as far as I found, been tabulated for stroke.
- Across systems the hospital share of stroke deaths (Brazil 78%, United States 43 to 47%, Sweden 47%, China 18%) bears no monotone relation to what is known about acute stroke admission in those countries. The cross-country level of H is dominated by where frail and dying people are cared for, so H cannot be pooled across regions without a covariate for nursing-home and die-at-home practice.
- The unspecified-stroke code is a useful internal check: home stroke deaths are disproportionately "unspecified" in Brazil (and 81.6% of out-of-hospital stroke deaths in Norway, see Key question 3), which suggests using the unspecified share of stroke deaths as a companion proxy for no acute imaging, and hence no acute admission.

### Gaps
- Could not verify: cause-specific place-of-death tabulations for Colombia (DANE), Chile (DEIS), South Africa (Stats SA), Egypt, Thailand, the Philippines, South Korea (KOSIS) and England (ONS). I confirmed only that Adair's database includes these countries (and holds cause-specific data for Chile, Colombia, Mexico, Uruguay). PubMed searches for stroke plus place of death in each country returned nothing usable.
- Could not verify: the meaning of the Mexican asist_medi codes (-1, 1, 2) and the official Brazilian Ministry of Health dictionary entry for ASSISTMED. One secondary source restricts ASSISTMED to deaths of women of reproductive age, which conflicts with its general position on the certificate; check before use.
- Caution on the Paraná abstract: read literally, 17.6% applies to ages 65+ and 29.8% to ages 45-64, yet the same abstract reports that place of death "was associated with age over 65 (OR= 1.69)". The Portuguese full text should be checked for which way round the figures go.
- WHO SCORE and the WHO Mortality Database were not examined; I found no indication in the sources read that either publishes place of death by cause.
- No cross-country compilation of place of death specifically for stroke was found.

## Key question 3. How strong is the caveat, especially in high-income countries with nursing-home deaths? Is there any study with both the proxy and the direct measure in the same population?

### Takeaway
The caveat is strong enough to rule out H as a level proxy in high-income countries: nursing homes account for 29 to 44% of stroke deaths in the United States and Sweden, so under half of stroke deaths occur in hospital although over 92% of registered incident strokes in Norway are hospitalised. The best paired evidence I found is small but direct: in Sichuan (2013), 90.1% of prevalent stroke survivors but only 70.0% of incident strokes (fatal and non-fatal) were hospitalised within 7 days, which shows survivor surveys overstate A by about 20 percentage points there.

### Inferences
- In high-income countries H is not usable as a level: roughly 45% of stroke deaths are in hospital while admission of incident strokes is above 90%. The complement of H is mostly nursing-home deaths, not strokes that never reached hospital.
- The quantity that does transfer from high-income linkage studies is the share of incident strokes that are out-of-hospital deaths with no admission (Norway 10.5% falling to 4.3%). It bounds A from above at about 90 to 96% in the best-served systems, because non-fatal strokes managed wholly outside hospital are invisible to those registers (the MONICA and England caveats).
- In China the three measures line up as H (18%) far below A (70%, small sample) below S (90%). If that ordering holds elsewhere, H and S bracket A, and the bracket is wide. A modelling strategy that uses H as a lower anchor and S as an upper anchor is defensible; using either alone is not.
- Whether fatal strokes are admitted more or less often than non-fatal ones is not settled by anything I found. Severity pushes fatal cases towards hospital; speed of death, fatalism ("incurable") and cost push them away. The Sichuan numbers (incident, including deaths, 70%; survivors 90%) imply that in that population fatal or early-dying cases were admitted less often than those who survived, which is the opposite of the severity argument.
- Cause-of-death composition offers a partial fix for high-income data: restricting H to deaths coded to acute stroke (I60-I64) and excluding sequelae (I69), or to deaths within 30 days of onset where the certificate or VA records duration, would remove much of the nursing-home dilution. None of the sources tabulated this.

### Gaps
- Could not verify: the MONICA table of the proportion of fatal and non-fatal events managed outside hospital by population (Beijing, Novosibirsk, Kaunas, Warsaw and others). The full text at ahajournals.org returned HTTP 403; only the abstract was read.
- Could not verify: the share of England's 947,497 stroke events that were deaths without admission. The figure was not in the passages or table rows extracted from the PMC full text of Seminog et al.
- Could not verify: the national NESS-China figure for hospitalisation within 7 days (the Sichuan paper implies the item was collected nationally). The Circulation paper is not in PMC and the publisher site was blocked. A Europe PMC full-text search for the Sichuan phrase found no sibling provincial papers.
- No study was found that reports H, C and A for the same population. The closest are Faridabad (H and C among deaths) and Sichuan (S and A).
- I found no published analysis restricting place of death to acute stroke codes or to deaths within 30 days of onset.

## Key question 4. Surveys of survivors: which large surveys ask people with self-reported stroke about hospitalisation or treatment, with what wording, and what has been published?

### Takeaway
None of the instruments I could open (LASI wave 1, WHO SAGE, the LASI pilot's HRS-style block) asks whether the respondent was hospitalised for the stroke; they ask about current medication and therapy, and separately record "Stroke/sudden paralysis of one side of body" as a coded reason for the last hospitalisation, which supports a hospitalisation-rate analysis but not a share of strokes admitted. India's NSS 2017-18 gives a stroke hospitalisation rate of 46 per 100,000 persons, about 0.42 of the GBD 2023 estimate of stroke incidence for India in 2017 (my ratio, with caveats), and the one survey found that did ask about acute hospitalisation (NESS-China, Sichuan) shows the survivor bias directly.

### Inferences
- Ratio for India (my calculation, not a published estimate): 46 stroke hospitalisations per 100,000 persons (NSS 2017-18) divided by 110.3 incident strokes per 100,000 (GBD 2023, year 2017) is 0.42, or 0.37 to 0.46 across the GBD uncertainty interval. This is an upper-leaning estimate of A if NSS admissions include recurrent strokes, readmissions and admissions for late effects under ailment code 25, and a lower-leaning one if admissions of people who died and whose households dissolved are missed. It is the only national, population-denominated number for India that I found, and it sits between Gadchiroli's 13% of stroke deaths outside the home and Faridabad's 56% of stroke deaths with hospital contact.
- The same rate construction is available, unpublished, from SAGE wave 1 in six countries (Q5005 to Q5008b, reason code 15, three-year and 12-month windows) and from LASI wave 1 (HC202 and HC208, reason code 19, 12-month window). Both identify only the most recent hospitalisation, so stroke admissions are undercounted when a later admission for another reason occurred; the bias is small for a 12-month window.
- Because LASI, SAGE, CHARLS, PNS and STEPS all condition on a diagnosis by a health professional, the population they describe has by construction touched the health system. Their treatment items (current medication, therapy, follow-up) measure continuing care, and say little about acute admission. SAGE's symptom items (Q4012, Q4013) are the exception: they allow a comparison of symptomatic but undiagnosed stroke with diagnosed stroke, which is a crude gauge of strokes that never reached a clinician.
- The direction of survivor bias, said in the brief to be unknown, has one data point: in Sichuan the hospitalised share was 20 percentage points higher among survivors than among all incident cases. The national NESS-China imaging figures (90% versus 83%) point the same way with a smaller gap. I would treat S as an upper bound on A in settings where early case fatality is high and admission is incomplete.
- The exit interviews of the HRS family and the CHARLS verbal autopsies cover deaths in nationally representative cohorts with place of death and hospital use before death, and they sit in the same projects as the survivor data. For China, the United States, Europe, Mexico, Korea and potentially India, they could supply H, C and S from one sampling frame, which no VA or registration system can do.

## Key question 5. Are there published estimates of unmet need or forgone care for stroke or acute cardiovascular events from these surveys, by country income group?

### Takeaway
I found no published estimate of forgone care specific to stroke or acute cardiovascular events by country income group. The Lancet Global Health Commission's amenable-mortality analysis states that condition-specific utilisation was unavailable for most conditions and was imputed from "conditions with similar clinical features", and the survey items that could fill the gap for stroke (SAGE Q5002 and Q5003a, LASI HC006 and HC007) exist but appear not to have been analysed for this purpose.

### Cited Findings
- Kruk et al. partitioned amenable deaths in 137 low- and middle-income countries into poor quality and non-utilisation: "8·6 million excess deaths were amenable to health care of which 5·0 million were estimated to be due to receipt of poor-quality care and 3·6 million were due to non-utilisation of health care." Ischaemic stroke (ages 0-74) is on the list of amenable conditions. — [Kruk et al., Lancet 2018, PMID 30195398, PMC6238021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6238021/)
- The same paper is candid about the utilisation input: "Because condition-specific utilisation measures were not available for all 61 conditions in LMICs, we used population utilisation data for conditions with similar clinical features and level of acuity. We used condition-specific utilisation data for tuberculosis, HIV, cancer, and mental health". Sources were "household population surveys and global estimates including the World Health Surveys, Demographic and Health surveys, UNICEF Multiple Indicator Cluster Surveys, World Mental Health Surveys", with missing countries imputed by regression on GDP per capita, rural share, female literacy, land area, health workers and GBD region. — [same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6238021/)
- SAGE records unmet need with stroke as a selectable reason: "Q5002 The last time you needed health care, did you get health care?", then "Q5003a. What was the main reason you needed care, even if you did not get care?" (code 15, stroke or sudden paralysis) and "Q5003b. Which reason(s) best explains why you did not get health care?" — [SAGE India Wave 1 Individual Questionnaire](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/SAGE-India-Wave1-Individual_Q.pdf)
- LASI records declined hospital referral for any cause: "HC006. ... In the past 12 months, did a health care provider ever recommend you to go to the hospital?" and "HC007. ... What were the reasons you decided against going to a hospital?" — [LASI W1 Individual Questionnaire](https://iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/LASI_W1_Individual_Questionnaire.pdf)
- Among fatal cases, forgone care is documented locally. Faridabad: "About half of the deceased did not visit any health facility during their terminal illness and only one in ten subjects reached an appropriate health facility within the first hour after onset of the symptoms"; delays in deciding to seek care in 38.4% ("60% due to non-recognition of seriousness") and in initiating treatment in 10.8% ("57% due to lack of affordability"). — [Krishnan et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lansea.2023.100222)
- Agincourt: of cardiovascular deaths reporting a delay on the WHO circumstances items, 74.8% (113/151) reported that "Costs of care and treatment prohibited other household payments", 16.7% that traditional medicine was used, and 10.6% that the person "Did not travel to a facility". — [BMJ Open 2021, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2020-048592)
- Mozambique (adults, all causes): of those who sought any outside care, 66.90% used formal care only, 25.33% both, 7.27% informal only, and formal care seeking rose with wealth ("highest household wealth (RRR: 3.1, P < 0.001)"). — [Am J Trop Med Hyg 2023](https://doi.org/10.4269/ajtmh.22-0548)
- After the acute phase, unmet rehabilitation need is measured nationally in Brazil: "only 24.6% reported having access to rehabilitation, while 73.4% of individuals with activity limitations received no physiotherapeutic treatment." — [Arq Neuropsiquiatr 2024](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0044-1792094)
- In India the financial barrier to stroke admission is quantified: "37% (34-40%) of households resorted to distress health financing due to stroke-related hospitalization." — [Rajasulochana and Kar 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/14737167.2021.1941883)

### Inferences
- The absence of a stroke-specific utilisation series is acknowledged in the most prominent prior attempt to split deaths into non-users and users of care, so the team's second factor fills a recognised hole. Kruk's regression covariates (GDP per capita, rural share, health workers, region) and Adair's (urbanisation, home births, health spending, education and income, region) are natural candidates for a model of A, and both papers show region effects that survive adjustment.
- SAGE wave 1 could yield, for six middle-income countries, the proportion of people whose last need for care was a stroke and who did not get care. Numbers will be small (stroke was the reason for few episodes) and the sample excludes those who died, so it would support an income-group contrast at best.
- Evidence on forgone acute care is strongest among decedents (Faridabad, Agincourt) and on forgone rehabilitation among survivors (Brazil). Nothing found measures forgone acute care among all incident strokes outside China.

### Gaps
- Could not verify: any analysis of the World Health Survey or SAGE unmet-need items restricted to stroke or acute cardiovascular events, or any income-group estimate of forgone stroke care. PubMed searches ("unmet need" or "forgone care" with stroke and the survey names) returned unrelated papers.
- Could not verify: whether PURE reports the share of incident cardiovascular events hospitalised by country income. The 2020 Lancet PURE paper (PMC8006904) contains no passage on hospitalisation in the text searched, and other PURE papers were not opened.
- Kruk et al.'s actual utilisation value applied to stroke, and which proxy condition it was borrowed from, are in the appendix, which was not retrieved.
- The China National Health Services Survey indicator of people advised to be hospitalised who were not admitted was not retrieved.
