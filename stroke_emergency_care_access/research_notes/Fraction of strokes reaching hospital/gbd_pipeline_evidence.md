## 1. What are the crosswalk rows for "Hospital", "Inpatient clinical informatics" and "USA claims", what is Equation 1, what is gamma, and what hospital-only/reference incidence ratio do they imply at ages 50, 70 and 85?

### Takeaway
The GBD 2023 hospital-only crosswalk says that studies restricted to strokes surviving to hospital admission see about 0.81 (ischaemic), 0.93 (intracerebral haemorrhage) and 0.87 (subarachnoid) of reference incidence at the model intercept, and (derived here) roughly 0.7 to 1.0 across ages 50 to 85, with the lowest values in old men with ischaemic stroke. Administrative inpatient and claims data run the other way, at 1.6 to 9.5 times reference, so they carry no usable signal about under-admission.

### Cited Findings

How hospital-based data enter the acute (first-ever) incidence models

- The reference case is population-based: "The reference definitions for ischaemic stroke and intracerebral haemorrhage were first-ever, subtype-specific stroke, which included subjects who did not survive to hospital admission. For these two subtypes we included, after adjustment, sources which used the following alternate definitions: 1) sources which included first and recurrent strokes; 2) sources which reported only estimates for all subtypes combined; and 3) sources which included only stroke cases which survived to hospital admission; 4) sources that were ascertained from administrative inpatient hospital data 5) sources that were ascertained from administrative hospital data in the USA." The subarachnoid reference likewise "included subjects who did not survive to hospital admissions", with alternates including "sources which included only stroke cases which survived to hospital admission". — [S23: Chronic stroke: Stroke cases are considered chronic beginning 28 days… (p.769)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- Three hospital-flavoured data streams are therefore distinguished: (a) literature or registry studies limited to cases surviving to admission (crosswalk label "Hospital", MR-BRT covariate name `cv_hospital` in the figure titles); (b) aggregate inpatient discharge data ("Inpatient clinical informatics", `cv_inpatient`); (c) US claims ("USA claims data"). Table 1 lists them as "Stroke cases that survived to hospital admission only", "Stroke cases that were ascertained from inpatient admission ICD codes" and "Stroke cases that were ascertained from inpatient admission ICD codes in the USA". — [S23: Chronic stroke… (p.769)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); figure titles read from the appendix PDF, printed pp. 778 and 782
- Inpatient data handling: "In addition to incidence data obtained from the literature reviews for acute stroke, we included inpatient hospital data, adjusted for readmission and primary to any diagnosis using correction factors estimated from claims data in the USA, Poland, Taiwan (province of China), and New Zealand. We excluded data for locations where the datapoints were implausibly low (Viet Nam, the Philippines, India, Nepal, China, Tibet, Kenya, Kyrgyzstan, Chile, Botswana, England, Brazil, Mexico, and Indonesia)." Unspecified stroke (I64) and I62 in hospital data are split to subtypes "according to the proportions of subtype-specific coded strokes in the inpatient hospital data by source". — [S23: Input data (p.771)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [S23: Input data (p.772)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- The crosswalk step: "We also adjusted data which included only persons who survived to hospital admission, using as reference data on both fatal and nonfatal strokes. In addition, we adjusted subtype-specific, inpatient clinical informatics data using subtype-specific literature estimates as a reference… We included a cubic spline constructed on a standardised age variable (age scaled) and a sex variable to the crosswalking procedure to adjust for variation by age and sex." — [S23: Input data (p.773)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- After crosswalking, the DisMod-MR incidence models carry no health-system covariate. Incidence has only the SEV scalar (for subarachnoid haemorrhage Table 7c lists systolic blood pressure in the without-CSMR model and an SEV scalar in the with-CSMR model, although the text says it "did not include an SEV covariate"); the access-type covariates sit on excess mortality: LDI in the "without CSMR" models and the HAQ Index in the "with CSMR" models. Published betas: acute ischaemic with CSMR, HAQ on excess mortality −0.015 (−0.016 to −0.014), exponentiated 0.95 (0.93 to 0.96); acute intracerebral haemorrhage −0.77 (−0.83 to −0.71), 0.46 (0.44 to 0.49); acute subarachnoid −0.95 (−1 to −0.86), 0.39 (0.37 to 0.42); incidence on log SEV scalar 0.87 (0.84 to 0.91) for ischaemic stroke. — [S23: Modelling strategy (p.804)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [S23: Modelling strategy (p.805)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [S23: Modelling strategy (p.806)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [S23: Modelling strategy (p.807)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); flowchart covariates "ln(LDI)" and "SEV" at [S23: Flowcharts (p.767)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)

Equation 1 and the age variable

- Equation 1 is garbled in the tool's text extraction, so I read it from the PDFs. GBD 2021 and GBD 2023 (identical): "Estimated Reference Def = invlogit(logit(Alternative Def) − [Σ_{s=0}^{b} Beta_{Alternative Def, spline basis s} ∗ Spline basis_s(age_scaled)] − Beta ∗ I(Sex))", with "I(.) = Indicator function, b = Number of spline bases used". — [S23: Input data (p.774)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); clean rendering in [GBD 2021 app. p.1061](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900757-8)
- GBD 2019 form (no spline): "Estimated Reference Def = invlogit(logit(Alternative Def) – Beta_Alternative Def – Beta_Sex * Sex – Beta_Age scaled * Age Scaled)". — [GBD 2019 app. p.838](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2930925-9); same text in [LN 2021 app. p.13](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422%2821%2900252-0)
- So beta = logit(alternative) − logit(reference), and the "adjustment factor" is exp(beta). The appendix's standard footnotes (printed under most crosswalk tables, though not under the stroke tables): "MR-BRT crosswalk adjustments can be interpreted as the factor the alternative case definition is adjusted by to reflect what it would have been had it been measured using the reference case definition. If the log/logit beta coefficient is negative, then the alternative is adjusted up to the reference…" and "The adjustment factor column is the exponentiated beta coefficient… For logit beta coefficients, this is the relative odds between the two case definitions." — GBD 2023 appendix 1 PDF, printed p.69 (first of 113 occurrences), [same DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- Standardisation: "We fit a cubic spline to the standardised age variable (named age_scaled), calculated as: age scaled = (mean age of study − mean(age of all studies)) / standard deviation(age of all studies)". The mean and SD are not printed. — [S23: Input data (p.774)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [GBD 2021 app. p.1061](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900757-8)

### Inferences
- Read at face value, the hospital-only crosswalk is GBD's estimate of the share of first-ever strokes that are captured by a study which only counts people admitted alive: the complement covers deaths before admission and non-fatal strokes never admitted. At face value that share is 80 to 95% for most of the age range, falling to about 70% in men aged 85 with ischaemic stroke.
- I would not lean on the age and sex detail. The intercepts moved between cycles (ischaemic −0.26, −0.08, −0.21; intracerebral +0.04, +0.11, −0.08 in GBD 2019, 2021, 2023), the knots were chosen by eye, and the fitted lines sit well above the plotted clouds.
- The gap between cloud and fit is consistent with a network meta-regression in which many plotted pairs compare hospital-only data with another alternative definition, not with the reference. For intracerebral haemorrhage, hospital minus clinical informatics would be −0.08 − 0.94 = −1.02 and hospital minus USA claims −1.71, which is where the cloud sits. The appendix does not say this; it is my explanation, and it means the scatter cannot be read as hospital versus reference.
- Hospital-only cohorts have about 30% lower 30-day excess mortality than the reference for ischaemic stroke (AF 0.70, with a CI that reaches zero on the logit scale), which is what one expects if the people missing from hospital cohorts include those who die before admission.
- Because incidence in DisMod has no health-system covariate, and the hospital crosswalk has no location term, nothing in the incidence model lets the hospital share differ between Japan and Somalia.

### Gaps
- Could not verify: the spline basis (tail linearity, which basis is dropped), so no exact evaluation of Tables 4a and 4b at a given age is possible; my age-specific values for ischaemic stroke and intracerebral haemorrhage rest on figure digitisation.
- Could not verify: the mean and SD used for age_scaled in the subarachnoid model (I assumed the 70.3 and 16.3 implied by the other two subtypes).
- Could not verify: units or transformation of the HAQ covariate in Table 7. The ischaemic row prints beta −0.015 with exponentiated beta 0.95, and exp(−0.015) is 0.985, while the haemorrhage rows (−0.77, 0.46) are self-consistent but far too large for a 0 to 100 index entered raw.
- Table 1 of the GBD 2023 write-up lists the USA inpatient alternate only for ischaemic stroke and no inpatient ICD alternate for subarachnoid haemorrhage, yet Tables 4b and 4c contain "USA claims data" and "Inpatient clinical informatics" rows for those subtypes. I report the tables.
- The GBD 2023 ischaemic table prints no beta for "Acute first-ever stroke, ages spline_3" in the tool's text (the PDF shows 0); this row does not matter for the hospital question.

## 2. Is the hospital-only crosswalk a single global relationship, and how many matched pairs or sources, from which countries, inform it?

### Takeaway
Yes: in GBD 2019, 2021 and 2023 the hospital-only adjustment is one global function of age and sex, with no location, year or health-system term, and none of the appendices I retrieved states how many matched pairs or which countries inform it. The incidence literature behind it is concentrated in high-income regions.

### Cited Findings
- The only covariates in the stroke crosswalk are the age spline and sex: "We included a cubic spline constructed on a standardised age variable (age scaled) and a sex variable to the crosswalking procedure to adjust for variation by age and sex." Tables 4a to 4c contain no location, year, income or HAQ rows. — [S23: Input data (p.773)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [S23: Input data (p.775)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [GBD 2021 app. pp.1060–1073](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900757-8)
- GBD 2019 used the same structure with linear age: "We also included a standardized age variable (age scaled) and a sex variable to the crosswalking procedure to adjust for the possibly of bias." — [GBD 2019 app. p.838](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2930925-9)
- How pairs are formed, in general: crosswalking involves "Finding pairs of alternative and reference… observations that match on relevant criteria (e.g. age, sex, location and year)"; "We compiled data from both within-study comparisons… and between-study comparisons (ie, data that used an alternative definition in one population and a reference definition in another population that overlap in location, time, age, and sex)… For between-study comparisons, we allowed a maximum calendar year difference between studies of five years"; "For quantities of interest with multiple alternative definitions/methods we also looked for pairs comparing two alternatives"; and network analysis is used "to leverage the additional information provided by indirect comparisons". Covariate choice: "we examined whether there were systematic differences in the adjustments by key demographics (age, sex, geographic location, year)… We did this when there was a strong rationale". — GBD 2023 appendix 1 PDF, Sections 2.3.1 to 2.3.4, printed pp. 30 to 34, [same DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- Between-study heterogeneity is handled as variance, not as structure: the adjusted data point's uncertainty adds "random intercepts in the meta-regression model (denoted γ above)". — GBD 2023 appendix 1 PDF, printed p.33, [same DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- Hospital data from most low- and middle-income locations are dropped before they can inform anything: the GBD 2021 and 2023 exclusion list is "Viet Nam, the Philippines, India, Nepal, China, Tibet, Kenya, Kyrgyzstan, Chile, Botswana, England, Brazil, Mexico, and Indonesia"; in GBD 2019 it was "Vietnam, Philippines, India". — [S23: Input data (p.771)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [GBD 2021 app. p.1059](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900757-8); [GBD 2019 app. p.837](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2930925-9)
- Where the incidence literature comes from (table "Counts of data used by measure and stroke model", first-ever acute ischaemic stroke, incidence sources by GBD region): Western Europe 27, North Africa and Middle East 9, Australasia 8, East Asia 5, Eastern Europe 5, High-income Asia Pacific 4, South Asia 4, Central Europe 3, Southern Latin America 2, Tropical Latin America 2, Central Asia 1, High-income North America 1, Eastern Sub-Saharan Africa 1, Southern Sub-Saharan Africa 1; zero in Southeast Asia, Oceania, Caribbean, Andean Latin America, Central Latin America, Central and Western Sub-Saharan Africa; total 73. ### Inferences
- Of the 73 ischaemic incidence sources in that table, 42 are from the high-income super-region and 2 from sub-Saharan Africa (my tally). Matched pairs need a reference study and a hospital-only study overlapping in location and time, so the hospital-only relationship is in practice a high-income relationship applied everywhere.
- The treatment of low admin counts as "implausibly low" rather than as evidence means the pipeline discards exactly the observations that would show under-admission in low- and middle-income countries. In data-poor countries modelled incidence is therefore driven by cause-specific mortality, the excess-mortality covariates (LDI or HAQ) and the SEV scalar, not by anything observed in hospitals.
- The table of counts by region labels its second panel "haemorrhagic stroke" and is identical in the 2021 and 2024 appendices, so I think it is carried over from an earlier cycle. I would use it for the geographic pattern, not for exact counts.
- The legacy "Hospital data" DisMod coefficients (1.46 and 1.71) have the opposite sign to the later "survived to hospital admission" crosswalk. This suggests the label once covered administrative inpatient data, and it shows how sensitive the sign is to what is called "hospital".

### Gaps

- Could not verify: whether location or year were tested as crosswalk covariates and rejected for stroke; the general text says only that such checks are made "when there was a strong rationale".

## 3. Exactly how is the HAQ adjustment applied to clinical (inpatient and claims) data, does it apply to stroke inputs, and what does it assume about under-utilisation where HAQ is low?

### Takeaway
The appendices give one identical sentence in GBD 2019, 2021 and 2023: every corrected inpatient estimate is divided by a HAQ-based scalar "to account for differences in access and quality of health care across time and space". The formula is not spelled out, and the stroke write-ups never mention it, so whether stroke inputs carry it rests on the general text alone.

### Cited Findings
- GBD 2023: "A final adjustment was applied to each of the above estimates. The HAQ Index was used to account for differences in access and quality of health care across time and space. The HAQ Index adjustment was applied by dividing the above estimates by a scalar ranging from 0 to 100, where 0 represents the first percentile of observed access and quality and 100 the 99th percentile." — [GBD 2023 Nonfatal methods appendix — Table 7. How correction factors are applied to create various incidence and… (p.29)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- The same three sentences appear in GBD 2021 and GBD 2019. — [GBD 2021 app. p.16](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900757-8); [GBD 2019 app. p.448](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2930925-9)
- "The above estimates" are the four clinical estimates: "(1) un-corrected inpatient admissions, primary diagnosis only; (2) inpatient admissions by individual encounter, deduplicated for readmissions, primary diagnosis only; (3) inpatient admissions by individual encounter, deduplicated for readmissions, accounting for all diagnoses; and (4) inpatient admissions and outpatient encounters by individual, deduplicated for readmissions, accounting for all diagnoses." "Estimate 4, where all three corrections are applied, was used for most causes, with exceptions based on the nature of the disease; in instances where outpatient care or non-primary diagnosis were not expected, estimates 3 or 2 were used instead." — [GBD 2023 Nonfatal methods appendix — Section 2.2.5: Correction Factors (p.23)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [(p.24)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- A cause write-up confirms the HAQ step is the fourth correction applied centrally: "Four rounds of data bias correction were employed in the processing of clinical data. This included 1) adjustment for readmission, 2) correction of primary diagnoses to all diagnoses, 3) adjustment for inpatient-to-outpatient ratio, and 4) adjustment based on Healthcare Access and Quality Index… a change made across GBD" (congenital anomalies). — [GBD 2021 app. p.276](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900757-8)
- The stroke write-up describes only "inpatient hospital data, adjusted for readmission and primary to any diagnosis", with no reference to HAQ. — [S23: Input data (p.771)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- The "0 = first percentile, 100 = 99th percentile" wording matches the HAQ Index's own scaling: "We transformed each cause to a scale of 0–100, with 0 as the first percentile (worst) observed between 1990 and 2016, and 100 as the 99th percentile (best)". — [HAQ 2016 paper, Lancet 2018;391:2236](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2818%2930994-2)
- IHME has itself suggested reading HAQ as a coverage fraction: "substantial interest exists in translating HAQ Index scores into coverage of populations or number of people with access to quality health services. Multiplying HAQ Index values by population could approximate this (ie, the 0–100 scale approximates 0–100%)". — [HAQ 2016 paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2818%2930994-2)
- Elsewhere in GBD 2023, HAQ is mapped directly to a treated or accessed share. Injuries: "To estimate the percentage treated for injuries in a given location-year, we used the Healthcare Access and Quality (HAQ) Index… We chose a reasonable cutoff for the HAQ Index (75 on a scale of 0–100) as the threshold at and above which 100% of injuries were treated. This value captured most OECD countries for all years back to 1980. We then scaled all remaining location-years between 10% and 100% treated based on their HAQ Index value". — [GBD 2023 Nonfatal methods appendix — Injuries: Modelling strategy (p.724)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- Chlamydia: "ProbRx = HAQ * (max probability - min probability) + (min probability)", with HAQ "scaled 0 to 1". Congenital syphilis: "In countries lower than the 75th percentile, we assume that the proportion of infants treated is equal to the HAQ Index value multiplied by the slope of treatment". Valve disease: "we ran a regression using the Healthcare Access and Quality (HAQ) Index predicting the level of treatment and set a prior that the proportion of individuals with a valve replacement or repair was zero where HAQ Index was equal to zero". — [GBD 2023 Nonfatal methods appendix — Sexually transmitted infections excluding HIV: Case definition (p.1185)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [— Sexually transmitted infections excluding HIV: Sequelae and disability weights (p.1180)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [— Non-rheumatic valvular diseases: Non-rheumatic valvular diseases (p.966)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- In the envelope that scales incomplete hospital sources, HAQ was a covariate in GBD 2019 (coefficient 0.029 per HAQ point for men, alongside log hospital beds 0.41 and all-cause mortality) and in GBD 2021 (near zero, −0.000039 for men, alongside log hospital beds 0.60 and log health expenditure per capita 0.21), and was removed in GBD 2023: "we replaced the Healthcare Access and Quality Index (HAQi) indicator with Sociodemographic Index (SDI)… to remove potential co-linearity between HAQi and healthcare utilization". — [GBD 2019 app. p.445](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2930925-9); [GBD 2021 app. pp.20–21](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900757-8); [GBD 2023 Nonfatal methods appendix — Changes from GBD 2021 to GBD 2023 (p.22)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)

### Inferences
- Dividing by a number between 0 and 100 only makes sense as dividing by HAQ/100, or by some rescaled version of it. If it is HAQ/100, the assumption is that the share of true cases appearing in inpatient data is proportional to the index: a country at HAQ 40 would have its hospital-derived rates multiplied by 2.5. That is a strong, cause-blind assumption, identical for stroke and for appendicitis. This reading is mine; the text does not give the formula.
- If the stroke inputs do carry this step, then two things follow. Part of the 1.6 to 3.5-fold excess of "inpatient clinical informatics" over reference could be this inflation (the included sources are mostly high-HAQ, where the factor would be about 1.1 to 1.3). And GBD already assumes, implicitly, that the hospitalised share of stroke scales with HAQ.
- The injuries rule (10% treated at the bottom of the HAQ range, 100% at HAQ 75 and above) is the closest thing in GBD to a published mapping from HAQ to a share receiving facility care. It is an assumption, not an estimate, but it is IHME's own and could be cited as a precedent for a HAQ-driven prior on the admitted fraction.

### Gaps
- Could not verify: the exact scalar (HAQ/100, a rescaled HAQ, or something else), and whether the stroke modellers used HAQ-adjusted or unadjusted clinical estimates. The stroke description (readmission plus primary-to-any diagnosis) matches "estimate 3" by wording, which is my matching, not a statement in the appendix.
- I found the "final adjustment" sentence in the GBD 2019, 2021 and 2023 appendices. A keyword search of the GBD 2017 nonfatal appendix ([Lancet 2018;392:1789](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2818%2932279-7), mmc1.pdf) did not find it, but I did not read that appendix in full.
- The processing flowchart (Figure S8, GBD 2023) shows no HAQ box, so the step is documented in text only.

## 4. Why would inpatient administrative data give 2.5 to 9 times the registry-based first-ever incidence, and what does the appendix say?

### Takeaway
The appendix gives no explanation; it only states that inpatient and claims data are adjusted to literature estimates. The documented processing rules (broad ICD code sets, any-diagnosis counting, and repeat encounters outside a duration window counted as new incident cases) are sufficient to produce large over-counts relative to first-ever stroke.

### Cited Findings
- The whole of the stated rationale: "In addition, we adjusted subtype-specific, inpatient clinical informatics data using subtype-specific literature estimates as a reference." — [S23: Input data (p.773)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- Size of the adjustment at the intercept: inpatient clinical informatics 1.55 (ischaemic), 2.56 (intracerebral), 3.46 (subarachnoid); US claims 2.43, 5.10, 9.47. — [S23: Input data (p.776)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [(p.781)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [(p.782)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [(p.784)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- ICD codes used for hospital and claims data (GBD 2023 Table 2): ischaemic stroke ICD-9 433–435.9, 437.0–437.1, 437.5–437.8 and ICD-10 I63–I63.9, I65–I66.9, I67.2–I67.3, I67.5–I67.6, I69.3; intracerebral haemorrhage 431–432.9, 437.2 and I61–I62, I62.1–I62.9, I68.1–I68.2, I69.1–I69.2; subarachnoid haemorrhage 430–430.9 and I60–I60.9, I62.0, I67.0–I67.1, I69.0. — GBD 2023 appendix 1 PDF, printed p.769, [same DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- GBD 2021's ischaemic list also included G45–G46.8. — [GBD 2021 app. p.1056](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900757-8)
- Incident cases in claims: "Incidence of a disease, impairment, or injury was calculated based on a duration window which varied by cause. Any individual who had multiple diagnoses for the same condition within the duration window is counted as a single incident case, and additional diagnoses outside of the duration window are treated as new incident cases." Claims sources "contain every health service encounter… including hospital inpatient records, with same-day inpatient setting visits and overnight admissions, and outpatient". — [GBD 2023 Nonfatal methods appendix — Section 2.2.2 Individual-level clinical data sources with unique identifiers… (p.14)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); full sentence read from the PDF, printed p.14
- Aggregate inpatient data: "Hospital inpatient records data were extracted from 2817 location-years in 44 countries… Counts of admissions with a primary diagnosis of each cause were extracted… Non-primary diagnostic detail, where available, was included through the correction factor modelling process". The non-primary correction increases "the estimate to include non-primary inpatient cases". — [GBD 2023 Nonfatal methods appendix — Figure S8. Processing steps for aggregate inpatient hospital admission sources (p.19)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [— Figure S12. Non-primary correction model in linear space for acute encephalitis (p.28)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- Correction factors come from a handful of systems: MarketScan, Medicare, Taiwan, Poland, PhilHealth, New Zealand and HCUP; "only MarketScan, Medicare, Poland, and the Taiwan claims data included a link between inpatient and outpatient care". Medicare (ages 65 and over; 2000, 2010, 2014 to 2016) was new in GBD 2023. — [GBD 2023 Nonfatal methods appendix — Figure S10. Overview of Correction Factor process (p.24)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); GBD 2023 appendix 1 PDF, printed pp. 14 to 15
- The reference they are compared with is first-ever stroke by WHO clinical criteria, with recurrent events treated as chronic stroke: "Chronic stroke includes the late sequelae of an acute stroke and all recurrent stroke events." — [S23: Chronic stroke… (p.769)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- For incomplete sources, counts are not observed counts at all: cause fractions are multiplied by a modelled all-cause admission envelope, "CFr = Acause/n; Atot = AR∗N; TAcause = CFr∗Atot". — [GBD 2023 Nonfatal methods appendix — Applying the inpatient utilization envelope to incomplete inpatient sources (p.23)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)

### Inferences
- My reading of why the ratios are so large, none of it stated by IHME: (1) the code lists go well beyond acute first-ever stroke, taking in sequelae codes (I69.x), occlusion and stenosis without infarction (I65 to I66), other cerebrovascular disease (I67.x, including unruptured aneurysm and dissection for subarachnoid haemorrhage), subdural and other non-traumatic intracranial haemorrhage (I62.x) under intracerebral haemorrhage, and ICD-9 433 to 435; (2) any diagnosis position counts after the non-primary correction; (3) recurrent strokes, and repeat encounters outside the duration window, count as new incident cases while the reference is first-ever only; (4) the HAQ division may add to it (Question 3).
- The pattern fits: the factor is largest for the rarest subtype and for claims (where outpatient and secondary codes abound), and for subarachnoid claims it climbs steeply with age (4.4 at 50 to 16.4 at 85, derived in Question 1), which is what miscoded and sequelae codes in the old would produce.
- For our purpose the consequence is simple. Administrative-to-registry ratios in GBD measure coding breadth and repeat counting, and cannot be inverted into a fraction of incident strokes reaching hospital.

### Gaps
- The appendix does not give the stroke-specific duration window, nor the stroke-specific readmission and non-primary correction factors (the worked figures are for acute encephalitis).
- Could not verify: which sources make up "USA claims data" for stroke (MarketScan, Medicare, HCUP or all three).

## 6. HAQ Index, UHC effective coverage index and its stroke component, inpatient utilisation envelope, SDI: definitions, coverage, public availability, and forecasts to 2050

### Takeaway
HAQ and a UHC index are available by country-year for 1980 to 2023 with uncertainty, stroke is one of HAQ's 32 causes, and the UHC stroke indicator is a rescaled mortality-to-incidence ratio; all of them are built from GBD's own stroke mortality and incidence estimates, so they are outcome proxies for access and not measurements of admission. I found no published IHME forecast of HAQ or UHC effective coverage to 2050; SDI is forecast internally to 2050, and health spending per capita is published to 2050.

### Cited Findings

HAQ Index

- Definition (2017): "We mapped the most widely used list of causes amenable to personal health care developed by Nolte and McKee to 32 GBD causes"; "we risk-standardised cause-specific mortality rates for each geography-year by removing the joint effects of local environmental and behavioural risks, and adding back the global levels of risk exposure"; "We employed principal component analysis to create a single, interpretable summary measure… on a scale of 0 to 100". Coverage: "195 countries and territories from 1990 to 2015". — [Lancet 2017;390:231](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2817%2930818-8)
- Stroke is one of the 32: Table 1 lists "Cerebrovascular disease | 0–74" under cardiovascular diseases (with rheumatic, ischaemic and hypertensive heart disease). — [Lancet 2017;390:231, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2817%2930818-8)
- 2018 update: "195 countries and territories, as well as subnational locations in seven countries, from 1990 to 2016"; "we used mortality-to-incidence ratios for cancers instead of risk-standardised death rates"; each cause scaled "with 0 as the first percentile (worst)… and 100 as the 99th percentile (best)"; PCA retained. — [Lancet 2018;391:2236](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2818%2930994-2)
- 2022 update: "HAQ Index construction methods were updated to use the arithmetic mean of scaled mortality-to-incidence ratios (MIRs) and risk-standardised death rates (RSDRs) for 32 causes"; "204 countries and territories between 1990 and 2019"; "the overall HAQ Index (ages 0–74 years)" plus "young (ages 0–14 years), working (ages 15–64 years), and post-working (ages 65–74 years)"; scaling is "separately for each age group". "Globally, the overall HAQ Index increased by 19·6 points between 1990 and 2019". — [Lancet Glob Health 2022;10:e1715](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X%2822%2900429-6)
- Stroke's component in the 2022 version is a risk-standardised death rate, not an MIR: Appendix Table 3 lists "Stroke 0-14, 15-64, 65-74" without the asterisk that marks "Mortality incidence ratio used instead of risk-standardized death rate", and stroke is absent from the list of causes tested for MIR. The three metabolic risks, including high systolic blood pressure, are excluded from risk standardisation "on the basis that they are measures that are amenable to personal healthcare". — [Lancet Glob Health 2022;10:e1715, appendix pp.16–20](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X%2822%2900429-6)
- Nothing above age 74 enters the index: the age limit is "(75 years) beyond which deaths were not amenable to health care used by Nolte and McKee". — [Lancet Glob Health 2022;10:e1715](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X%2822%2900429-6)
- Availability, verified: the GBD 2023 covariate "Healthcare access and quality index" (covariate_id 1099, short name haqi, "index (0-100)") is served for 1980 to 2023 and 934 locations, both sexes, all ages, with lower and upper bounds. UHC effective coverage index

- Construction: "we assessed UHC effective coverage for 204 countries and territories from 1990 to 2019"; 23 effective coverage indicators; "outcome-based measures were transformed to values on a scale of 0–100 based on the 2·5th and 97·5th percentile of location-year values"; the index weights indicators by "health gains fraction". — [Lancet 2020;396:1250](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2930750-9)
- The stroke indicator (Table 1): "Stroke treatment | MIR | Mortality from stroke for populations aged 20–64 years | Incidence of stroke for populations aged 20–64 years", effectiveness category 2; and the same for "populations aged ≥65 years", effectiveness category 3. Appendix wording: "Treatment of stroke: proxied using rescaled death to incidence ratio for stroke". Scaling: "0 was set by values at the 97·5th percentile or higher (ie, 'worst' levels of MIRs) and 100 by the 2·5th percentile or lower". — [Lancet 2020;396:1250, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2930750-9); appendix 1 p.21 of the same paper
- The authors' own caution about such indicators: "few data sources can adequately represent these dimensions for conditions involving more complex care (eg, cancer or stroke)". — [Lancet 2020;396:1250](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2930750-9)
- What is in the paper's appendix 2: health gain weights by indicator and location for 2019 (Supplementary table 1) and index values for 1990, 2010 and 2019 (Supplementary table 2). Indicator values by country are not tabulated there. — [Lancet 2020;396:1250, appendix 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2930750-9)
- Indicator-level data, from a search-engine summary only: the GHDx record "GBD 2019 UHC Effective Coverage Index 1990-2019" offers "UHC Effective Coverage Index and indicator estimates" (CSV) for 204 countries "in 1990, 2010, and 2019", plus indicator weights. — [GHDx record](https://ghdx.healthdata.org/record/ihme-data/gbd-2019-uhc-effective-coverage-index-1990-2019)
- Annual series, verified: the GBD 2023 covariate "Universal health coverage" (covariate_id 1097; "Coverage of universal health coverage tracer interventions for prevention and treatment services, percent; created for GBD 2015 SDGs paper"; index 0 to 100) is served for 1980 to 2023 and 934 locations with bounds. Values for 2023: Japan 96.4, United States 85.7, China 68.5, India 54.4, Nigeria 34.0, Somalia 25.3. — IHME_GBD MCP tool `get_covariate`, citation "Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME). Global Burden of Disease Study 2023"
- The older UHC service coverage index contains HAQ: it "includes nine measures of coverage… and the 32 causes that comprise the Healthcare Access and Quality (HAQ) Index". — [GBD 2017 SDG paper, Lancet 2018;392:2091](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2818%2932281-5)

SDI

- "The Socio-demographic Index (SDI) is a composite indicator of background social and economic conditions… it is the geometric mean of 0 to 1 indices of total fertility rate (TFR) for those younger than 25 years old (TFU25), mean education for those 15 years old and older (EDU15+), and lag-distributed income (LDI) per capita. After calculating SDI, values were multiplied by 100 for a scale of 0 to 100." — [GBD 2023 Nonfatal methods appendix — Section 3.1: SDI definition (p.60)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- LDI is also served as a covariate: "Lag distributed income per capita (I$): gross domestic product per capita that has been smoothed over the preceding 10 years", 1950 to 2023, 934 locations. — IHME_GBD MCP tool `get_covariate` catalogue, GBD 2023

Inpatient utilisation envelope (all-cause admissions per person per year)

- GBD 2023: "all-cause inpatient admission rate estimates from 1990 to 2019 for all age groups including <1 year detail, males and females, and 934 locations"; "We defined a hospital admission as hospitalization in a formal health care facility for, at least, an overnight stay"; modelled "with Spatiotemporal Gaussian Process Regression (ST-GPR) adjusting for total health expenditure per capita, log hospital beds per 1,000 population, and Sociodemographic Index (SDI)"; inputs include "over 2,000 location-years of government reports", Eurostat, HFA-DB and OECD. — [GBD 2023 Nonfatal methods appendix — Section 2.2.4: Estimation of the inpatient utilization envelope (p.20)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [— Case definition (p.20)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [— Table 5. Inclusion and exclusion criteria for data sources used in the… (p.21)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [— Changes from GBD 2021 to GBD 2023 (p.22)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- A published, downloadable version exists for an earlier cycle: "we produced estimates of utilisation per person for visits and admissions by age and sex for 195 countries from 1990 to 2016", from "2068 country-years of inpatient data from 128 countries", with "hospital beds per 1000 population in the inpatient model"; in 2016 "the inpatient utilisation rate was 0·10 admissions (0·09–0·11) per capita" globally, and "Bulgaria had the highest inpatient utilisation rate (0·27, 0·26–0·28) and Cambodia had the lowest (0·02, 0·02–0·03)". "To download the data used in these analyses, please visit the Global Health Data Exchange at http://ghdx.healthdata.org/node/380336." — [Moses et al., Lancet Public Health 2019;4:e49](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667%2818%2930213-5)
- The envelope is not among the covariates served by the GBD 2023 tool; the catalogue has 27 covariates and no hospital beds, admissions or health expenditure series. — IHME_GBD MCP tool `get_covariate` catalogue, GBD 2023

Forecasts to 2050

- GBD 2021 forecasting covers "2022 to 2050 for 204 countries and territories" for mortality, YLLs, YLDs and DALYs. SDI is forecast as an input: "we standardised education… LDI, and ASFR, and took the geometric mean to obtain location-specific forecasts of the Socio-demographic Index (SDI), used as a driver for mortality and non-fatal models". — [Lancet 2024;403:2204](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900685-8)
- Stroke incidence in those forecasts is not modelled directly. Appendix Table S3 gives ischaemic stroke "MIR" for incidence and "Direct Prevalence" for prevalence, and intracerebral and subarachnoid haemorrhage "MIR" and "PIR". Method: "Ratios of MI, MP, PI… are all modelled via log(R) = β0 + β1 SDI + [age-sex-location random intercept and random slope on SDI]"; "the MI and MP ratios were converted to incidence and prevalence, respectively, via division of mortality forecasts by the corresponding ratio". — [Lancet 2024;403:2204, appendix 1 pp.37–38 and Table S3 p.64](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900685-8)
- HAQ and UHC do not appear as forecast quantities. The main text never mentions the HAQ Index; the methods appendix mentions it once, as a covariate for vaccine scale-up curves ("modelled as a function of healthcare access and quality"). — [Lancet 2024;403:2204, appendix 1 p.13](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900685-8)
- Where forecast outputs are published: "To download forecasted estimates used in these analyses, please visit the GBD Foresight visualisation tool". The tool's page describes itself as "forecasts and better and worse scenarios from 2022 to 2050 for both causes of death and risks, as measured by years of life lost (YLLs), and total deaths", release "GBD 2021", year range 1990 to 2050. Its data API answered 401 without a login. — [Lancet 2024;403:2204](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900685-8); [GBD Foresight](https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-foresight/)
- UHC projections that do exist are short-range: the 2020 paper estimates "additional population equivalents with UHC effective coverage from 2018 to 2023"; the GBD 2017 SDG paper "projected indicators to 2030" for 195 countries, including the UHC service coverage index; the 2018 health financing paper relates pooled spending to a "UHC index" under scenarios to 2030 and 2040 for 188 countries. — [Lancet 2020;396:1250](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2930750-9); [Lancet 2018;392:2091](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2818%2932281-5); [Lancet 2018;391:1783](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2818%2930697-4)
- Health spending is published to 2050: "estimate total health spending from 1995 to 2050" for "204 countries and territories", with "Spending on health… expected to rise to $1519 (1448–1591) per person in 2050"; details "on the Global Health Data Exchange website at http://ghdx.healthdata.org/series/financing-global-health-fgh". An earlier round covered "195 countries, 1995–2050". — [Lancet 2021;398:1317](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2821%2901258-7); [Lancet 2019;393:2233](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2819%2930841-4)
- The Commission forecast of stroke burden to 2050 uses the same machinery: mortality "modelled as a function of the Socio-demographic Index and time", a risk factor scalar, and residual mortality; "stroke mortality will increase by 50%—from 6·6 million… in 2020, to 9·7 million… in 2050". — [Lancet Neurol 2023;22:1160](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422%2823%2900277-6)

### Inferences
- All three access indices are circular with respect to stroke. HAQ's stroke component is risk-standardised stroke mortality under 75; the UHC stroke indicator is stroke deaths over stroke incidence; and stroke incidence in data-poor countries is itself produced by a model in which excess mortality depends on HAQ. They rank health systems sensibly, but they contain no observation of who was admitted.
- HAQ's age ceiling of 74 is awkward for a stroke-unit analysis, since a large share of strokes occur above it. The post-working (65 to 74) HAQ is the closest age-specific version, published for 1990 to 2019.
- For projection to 2050 the practical route is to forecast the covariate oneself. SDI forecasts exist inside IHME but I could not confirm a public file. Health spending per capita to 2050 is published and is one of the three covariates in IHME's own inpatient envelope model. A HAQ-on-SDI or HAQ-on-spending relationship fitted on 1990 to 2023 would be a crude bridge, and it would inherit the assumption that HAQ keeps tracking development as it has.
- The GBD forecast of incident strokes (our first factor) is forecast deaths divided by an SDI-driven mortality-to-incidence ratio. Any covariate for the admitted fraction that is also driven by SDI will be strongly correlated with the first factor's own driver, which is worth knowing before multiplying them.
- The all-cause admissions envelope is the only one of these that measures hospital use directly. It says how often people are admitted for anything, not whether a stroke is admitted, but the 13-fold range between Cambodia and Bulgaria in 2016 is the kind of variation the HAQ-style indices only imply.

## 7. Is there any GBD or IHME publication that directly estimates the share of stroke (or acute cardiovascular) cases that are hospitalised, by country?

### Takeaway
I found none. The closest items are a non-IHME analysis that borrows a survey care-seeking proxy for stroke (88 to 92% in low- and middle-income countries), IHME's all-cause admission rates, and IHME's own HAQ-to-treated-share rules for other causes.

### Cited Findings
- Kruk and colleagues (Harvard; uses GBD 2016 inputs) split amenable deaths into non-utilisation and poor quality using survey utilisation: "Because condition-specific utilisation measures were not available for all 61 conditions in LMICs, we used population utilisation data for conditions with similar clinical features and level of acuity." For ischaemic stroke and intracerebral haemorrhage the measure is "Other acute and child acute illness utilization", defined as "Sought care at in- or outpatient facility" among those who "Needed health care for self in the past year for asthma, heart disease or minor surgery" (World Health Survey; 40 LMICs with data; averages 88% low-income, 88% lower-middle, 92% upper-middle), imputed elsewhere from GDP per capita, rurality, female literacy, land area and health workers. "Because the data on service utilisation from World Health Surveys were from 2002–03, we conducted a sensitivity analysis". Result: "Cardiovascular disease deaths made up 33% (2 817 000) of the amenable deaths… of which 84% (2 358 000) were caused by use of poor-quality health services." — [Lancet 2018;392:2203 and appendix Tables S2–S3, pp.5–6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2818%2931668-4)
- IHME's utilisation paper estimates admissions for all causes combined, by age, sex, country and year, not by disease. — [Lancet Public Health 2019;4:e49](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667%2818%2930213-5)
- Within GBD, ischaemic heart disease has the same construct as stroke: an alternate definition for acute myocardial infarction that "Only includes persons who survived to the hospital", and a model of "the proportion of IHD deaths associated with a diagnosis of myocardial infarction within 30 days of death with age, sex, year, and the Healthcare Access and Quality (HAQ) Index as linear covariates", fitted on linked data from New Zealand, Italy, Sweden, England and Spain. — [GBD 2023 Nonfatal methods appendix — Ischaemic heart disease: Case definition (p.746)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); [— Ischaemic heart disease: Modelling strategy (p.759)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- GBD's own shares of cases treated, where it needs them, are HAQ rules, not measurements (injuries 10% to 100% with a cutoff at HAQ 75; chlamydia; congenital syphilis; valve disease). — [GBD 2023 Nonfatal methods appendix — Injuries: Modelling strategy (p.724)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X); other citations in Question 3
- GBD defines some quantities explicitly as need regardless of access: COVID-19 hospitalised cases are "cases of COVID-19 needing hospitalisation but not ICU care, regardless of access to or utilisation of care". — [GBD 2023 Nonfatal methods appendix — COVID-19: Hospitalised and ICU cases (p.390)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)
- The World Stroke Organization–Lancet Neurology Commission, which uses IHME forecasts, treats the gap as a surveillance problem and recommends that "Governments should establish national stroke registries of hospitalised, non-hospitalised, fatal, and non-fatal strokes and transient ischaemic attacks"; I found no country estimates of the hospitalised share in it. — [Lancet Neurol 2023;22:1160](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422%2823%2900277-6)
- The UHC paper states the general difficulty: "most health data systems are not able to capture all three intervention components together (ie, need, use or receipt, and quality)". — [Lancet 2020;396:1250](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2930750-9)

### Inferences
- The hypothesis that evidence for the admitted fraction is embedded in the GBD stroke inputs holds only in a narrow sense. What is embedded is one global, age-and-sex-specific ratio of about 0.7 to 1.0, learned mostly where almost everyone is admitted, plus a set of HAQ-based conventions that IHME uses when it needs a treated share. There is no country-specific or time-specific evidence about stroke admission anywhere in the pipeline.
- A defensible use of the GBD material is as an upper anchor: where health systems resemble those that produced the matched pairs, about 80 to 95% of incident strokes are in a hospital-only count. Country variation below that anchor has to come from outside GBD (registries and surveys, which other notes cover) or from an assumed link to HAQ, admissions per capita or spending. I would state that link as an assumption, since GBD does.
- Kruk's 88 to 92% is care-seeking at any facility, inpatient or outpatient, for asthma, heart disease or minor surgery, among people who said they needed care, in 2002 to 2003. It is a poor stand-in for acute stroke admission and almost certainly too high for that purpose, but it is the only published cross-country number I found that has been attached to stroke in a GBD-based analysis.
- The three GBD streams bracket the truth badly: hospital-only literature (0.7 to 1.0 of reference), administrative data (1.6 to 9.5 times reference), and excluded "implausibly low" administrative data. None can be turned into a country-year admitted fraction without outside data.
