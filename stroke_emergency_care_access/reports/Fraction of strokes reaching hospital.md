# GBD anchors but cannot map stroke hospital reach

The GBD stroke pipeline holds one piece of evidence on the fraction of incident strokes that reach hospital: the crosswalk that adjusts studies of cases who "survived to hospital admission" up to a population-based reference. It goes less far than the calculation needs, because it is a single global function of age and sex, with no location, year or health-system term, whose GBD 2023 intercepts put hospital-only incidence at **0.81 (ischaemic), 0.93 (intracerebral haemorrhage) and 0.87 (subarachnoid) of reference incidence** ([GBD 2023 methods appendix](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)). Read by age it implies roughly 0.7 to 1.0 between ages 50 and 85 (a derivation in the notes, not a GBD estimate), and as far as the published source counts show it was learned mostly where nearly everyone is admitted, so it is an upper anchor for well-resourced systems and says nothing about Somalia that it does not also say about Japan. Direct population-based measurements of the admitted share run from **6.9% of first-ever strokes in a rural Chinese township in 1992-2000 to 98.9% of stroke events in Auckland in 2021-22** ([Han 2021](https://doi.org/10.2147/RMHP.S325822); [Feigin 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanwpc.2025.101508)), and **no direct estimate exists for sub-Saharan Africa or for North Africa and the Middle East**. Most incident strokes occur where this evidence is thinnest: of 13,238,353 incident strokes globally in 2023, 5,853,726 were in Southeast Asia, East Asia and Oceania and 2,317,083 in South Asia ([IHME GBD 2023 results tool](https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results/)), so China and India dominate the calculation while resting on a handful of study sites. The Cochrane trials enrolled only people already admitted to hospital, most within 7 days of onset, and excluded subarachnoid haemorrhage ([Cochrane CD000197.pub4](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000197.pub4)), so the factor must be defined as incident ischaemic and intracerebral strokes admitted as inpatients in the acute phase, which is narrower than any hospital contact. The path the notes support is a logit meta-regression of the study-level admitted share on a development or access covariate, with the definitional traps coded as design covariates. It would be anchored at the top by the GBD crosswalk and by linked-register countries, and bracketed in data-poor regions by decedent proxies from below and survivor surveys from above. The first steps are cheap: fix the definition in writing, pull the crosswalk's matched pairs and the excluded "implausibly low" hospital data inside IHME, and use library access to close the paywalled gaps for China and India before the others.

## The Cochrane trials fix the definition: admitted within about a week, not any hospital contact

A convention for what follows: a number with a citation is established in that source, "derived in the notes" marks arithmetic on published values (which is not a published estimate), and judgments are labelled as the notes' or as mine.

The fourth factor decides how the second must be defined, so I start there. The Cochrane review of organised inpatient (stroke unit) care pools 29 trials with 5,902 participants. At a median follow-up of one year it reports odds ratios of **0.76 (95% CI 0.66 to 0.88) for death**, 0.76 (0.67 to 0.85) for death or institutional care and 0.75 (0.66 to 0.85) for death or dependency ([Cochrane CD000197.pub4](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000197.pub4)). Its eligibility rule is a single sentence, "Any person admitted to hospital who had suffered a stroke was eligible", with a clinical definition of stroke "excluding subarachnoid haemorrhage and subdural haematoma". Most trials randomised within 7 days of onset, but the review counts eight units that admitted participants "after a delay of one or two weeks", and several trials selected on fitness for rehabilitation. Outcomes did not differ by age, sex or stroke type. The one significant subgroup interaction was severity (P = 0.004), with larger benefit in more severe stroke, and for mild stroke the benefit held for death or dependency but "was less certain" for death. The trials come from 12 countries, and the only ones outside high-income settings are five Chinese trials and one Brazilian trial, with none from a low-income or lower-middle-income country (the country assignment of the other trials is the notes' reading of their place names).

Three consequences follow, and they are inferences from the trial population, not statements in the review. First, the denominator that matches the effect size is incident ischaemic and intracerebral strokes admitted to a hospital bed within roughly the first week, so the first factor should drop subarachnoid haemorrhage from the GBD forecast before anything is multiplied. Second, three boundary cases need a written decision. Strokes assessed in an emergency department or rapid-access clinic and sent home are absent from the trials. Strokes with onset in a patient already in hospital are eligible in the trials' sense but do not "reach" hospital, and they were **11.7% of hospital-treated first-ever strokes in South London** ([Addo 2011](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.d744)). Strokes admitted only later for rehabilitation were eligible in seven or eight of the 29 trials. Third, a pooled effect that mixes acute and post-acute enrolment is conditional on surviving to enrolment, so applying 0.76 to everyone who reaches the hospital door slightly overstates the eligible base where early in-hospital deaths are common. If the "stroke rehabilitation" arm of the scale-up means early supported discharge or therapy at home, it needs its own base and its own effect size, since those trials recruited hospital survivors with mild to moderate disability and found no effect on death (odds ratio 1.04, 95% CI 0.77 to 1.40) ([Cochrane CD000443.pub4](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000443.pub4)).

The same definition has to hold across the second and third factors, because national audits report the stroke-unit share among the admitted while population registers report it among all strokes. In South London between 1995-97 and 2007-09 the admitted share of first-ever strokes rose from 82.1% to 94.7%, while the stroke-unit share among the admitted rose from 18.9% to 78.4% ([Addo 2011](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.d744)), so most of the gain in population coverage came from the stroke-unit term. GBD's acute incidence models are for first-ever stroke by definition, with "all recurrent stroke events" counted under chronic stroke ([GBD 2023 methods appendix](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)). That the forecast of incident strokes inherits this is an inference to confirm with the forecasting group, since recurrent strokes admitted to hospital are eligible in the trials' sense and are counted by the audits, yet would be absent from the base.

Varna, Bulgaria 2000-01: 76% in urban men under 65 against 15% in rural women over 65 ([Powles 2002](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2458-2-24)) | Code urban or rural; attach subnational covariate values where they exist |
| Age windows | East German MONICA 1985-88: 10% treated outside hospital at ages 25 to 64 against 26% at 65 to 74 ([Eisenblätter 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8164773/)). | Case-finding that runs through facilities | Lagos 2007: an 84.6% "hospitalization rate" among cases presenting to health facilities, with pre-hospital deaths excluded ([Danesi 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jns.2013.04.026)). India's national registry programme "focused on cases that had some form of medical attendance" ([Rangamani 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lansea.2023.100308)) | Treat as upper bounds, not as estimates |
| Decedent and survivor proxies that bracket the truth | Sichuan 2013: 90.1% of prevalent survivors but 70.0% of incident strokes hospitalised within 7 days ([Front Neurol 2020](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2020.00877)). China nationally, 2008-20: 17.6% of stroke deaths in medical institutions (derived in the notes from published counts) ([Lancet Reg Health West Pac 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanwpc.2022.100383)) | Use as an upper and a lower bracket, never alone |

## GBD embeds one global age-sex ratio near 0.7 to 1.0, with no country or time signal

Everything about GBD in this section was read from the published GBD 2019, 2021 and 2023 methods appendices and the appendices of the two GBD stroke papers. The IHME intranet (the Hub), the IHME shared drives and the GHDx website (which sits behind a browser challenge) were not reachable from the research environment, so anything that needs the stroke bundle, the matched pairs behind the crosswalk or the excluded clinical data remains to be pulled inside IHME. The last part of this section lists it.

### What the appendices establish

The reference case definition for ischaemic stroke and intracerebral haemorrhage is "first-ever, subtype-specific stroke, which included subjects who did not survive to hospital admission" ([GBD 2023 methods appendix](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)). Three hospital-flavoured alternates are crosswalked to it: literature or registry studies "which included only stroke cases which survived to hospital admission" (the "Hospital" rows, covariate `cv_hospital` in the figure titles), aggregate inpatient discharge data ("Inpatient clinical informatics") and US claims. The crosswalk is an MR-BRT network meta-regression on the logit difference between alternative and reference, and its only covariates are a cubic spline on standardised age and a sex term. Tables 4a to 4c contain no location, year, income or HAQ rows, and GBD 2019 used the same structure with linear age ([GBD 2019 methods appendix](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2930925-9)). The GBD 2023 intercepts are below, as logit beta (95% CI) and the printed adjustment factor.

| Subtype | "Hospital" (survived to admission) | Inpatient clinical informatics | US claims | Gamma (incidence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ischaemic stroke | −0.21 (−0.33 to −0.09); 0.81 | 0.44 (0.34 to 0.54); 1.55 | 0.89 (0.76 to 1.02); 2.43 | 0.063 |
| Intracerebral haemorrhage | −0.08 (−0.29 to 0.13); 0.93 | 0.94 (0.80 to 1.08); 2.56 | 1.63 (1.44 to 1.82); 5.10 | 0.12 |
| Subarachnoid haemorrhage | −0.14 (−0.28 to −0.01); 0.87 | 1.24 (1.11 to 1.37); 3.46 | 2.25 (2.10 to 2.40); 9.47 | 0.10 |

After the crosswalk, the DisMod-MR incidence models carry no health-system covariate. Incidence has only a risk-exposure covariate (the SEV scalar, or systolic blood pressure in one subarachnoid model), and the access-type covariates (LDI in the models without CSMR, the HAQ Index in those with it) sit on excess mortality. Hospital data from many low- and middle-income locations never reach the stroke model, since GBD 2021 and GBD 2023 "excluded data for locations where the datapoints were implausibly low (Viet Nam, the Philippines, India, Nepal, China, Tibet, Kenya, Kyrgyzstan, Chile, Botswana, England, Brazil, Mexico, and Indonesia)". The general clinical-data text also describes a "final adjustment" in which each corrected inpatient estimate is divided by a HAQ-based scalar "to account for differences in access and quality of health care across time and space". The formula is not given, and the stroke write-up mentions only readmission and primary-to-any-diagnosis corrections. The literature base was last reviewed systematically for GBD 2021 (search to 25 February 2020), and "A systematic review was not performed for stroke in GBD 2023". **None of the three appendices states how many matched pairs, which sources or which countries inform the "Hospital" comparison** (all from the [GBD 2023 methods appendix](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X), with the earlier cycles checked in the [GBD 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900757-8) and [GBD 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2930925-9) appendices).

### How far it goes, and where inference begins

Read at face value, the "Hospital" coefficient is GBD's estimate of the share of first-ever strokes captured by a study that counts only people admitted alive, which is the right quantity for this purpose. The complement covers both deaths before admission and non-fatal strokes never admitted. The age-specific values below are derived in the notes and are not GBD estimates. The appendix does not publish the spline basis, so the fitted lines in its Figures 2b and 2f were digitised (reading error about ±3% on the ratio). The subarachnoid values come from the linear formula under an assumed age standardisation (mean 70.3, SD 16.3, recovered from the published knots for the other two subtypes).

| Implied hospital-only to reference ratio (derived in the notes) | Age 50 | Age 70 | Age 85 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ischaemic stroke, female | 0.99 | 0.94 | 0.83 |
| Ischaemic stroke, male | 0.82 | 0.78 to 0.80 | 0.69 to 0.70 |
| Intracerebral haemorrhage, either sex | 1.00 to 1.02 | 0.93 to 0.95 | 0.91 |
| Subarachnoid haemorrhage, female | 1.00 | 0.87 | 0.79 |

I would not lean on the age and sex detail, for reasons that are visible in the appendices themselves. The intercepts moved between cycles (ischaemic −0.26, −0.08, −0.21 and intracerebral +0.04, +0.11, −0.08 in GBD 2019, 2021 and 2023), and the GBD 2019 ischaemic interval ran from −2.22 to 1.70. The knots were chosen "based on visual inspection of the spline fit". In the published figures the fitted lines sit well above the plotted clouds: for intracerebral haemorrhage most points lie between about −0.8 and −2.5, while the fitted line sits between +0.25 and −0.15. The notes explain this, as their own inference, by network pairs that compare hospital-only data with claims or inpatient data and not with the reference. Reading the single network-wide gamma as a variance, as the appendix defines it, the notes derive a 95% range of study-level ratios of about ×0.61 to ×1.64 around the ischaemic fit. An older DisMod-internal coefficient labelled "Hospital data" even has the opposite sign (1.46 and 1.71) ([GBD 2019 stroke paper appendix](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422%2821%2900252-0)), which shows how sensitive the sign is to what is called "hospital".

The geography is the larger limit. A published count table for first-ever ischaemic stroke lists 73 literature incidence sources, 27 of them from Western Europe and none from Southeast Asia, Oceania, the Caribbean, Andean or Central Latin America, or Central and Western sub-Saharan Africa. By the notes' tally 42 are from the high-income super-region and 2 from sub-Saharan Africa. The table appears to be carried over from an earlier cycle, so it is good for the pattern and not for exact counts ([GBD 2019 stroke paper appendix](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422%2821%2900252-0)). A matched pair needs a hospital-only study and a reference study that overlap in place and time, so the relationship is in practice a high-income one applied everywhere. That is inference, and the pull below would confirm or refute it.

The administrative streams cannot be turned around to help. Inpatient and claims data run at 1.55 to 9.47 times reference, and the appendix gives no explanation. The notes' reading is that broad ICD code sets (including sequelae codes), any-diagnosis counting and repeat encounters counted as new incident cases are sufficient to produce the excess, so these ratios measure coding breadth and not under-admission. The exclusion list has the same effect from the other side, as the notes read it, since treating low administrative counts as implausible discards exactly the observations that would show under-admission. One caution comes from the notes' own arithmetic. China's Hospital Quality Monitoring System recorded 3,807,944 ischaemic, intracerebral and subarachnoid admissions in 1,599 hospitals in 2019 ([Tu 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.archger.2022.104832)), against 3,765,657 incident strokes in GBD 2023 for China in the same year ([IHME GBD 2023 results tool](https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results/)). Either the files GBD holds are less complete than these, or "implausibly low" was judged against an expectation of several admissions per incident case, and the appendix does not say which.

So the answer to the first question is yes, in a narrow sense. What is embedded is one global, age-and-sex-specific ratio of about 0.7 to 1.0, learned mostly where almost everyone is admitted, together with HAQ-based conventions that GBD uses when it needs a treated share. For injuries the rule is "a reasonable cutoff for the HAQ Index (75 on a scale of 0–100) as the threshold at and above which 100% of injuries were treated", with the remaining location-years scaled "between 10% and 100% treated". For myocardial infarction it is a logit-space model of the proportion of infarctions reaching care, with HAQ, age and their interaction among its covariates, informed by linked hospital and death data from five high-income sources ([GBD 2023 methods appendix](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)). There is no country-specific or time-specific evidence about stroke admission anywhere in the published pipeline. The defensible use is as an upper anchor: where health systems resemble those that produced the matched pairs, about 80% to 95% of incident strokes appear in a hospital-only count.

### High-income countries and Europe: saturated where admission is the policy

For high-income countries, Europe and the former Soviet Union the notes assemble 52 study-periods. The admitted share runs from 34% (Tartu, first-ever strokes, 1970-73) to 98.9% (Auckland, all events admitted within 28 days, 2021-22) ([Vibo 2007](https://digar.ee/arhiiv/et/download/233212); [Feigin 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanwpc.2025.101508)). Every site with repeated waves that started low rose. Auckland went from 62.5% in 1981-82 through 72.5%, 90.9% and 90.9% to 98.9%, South London from 82% in 1995-2000 to 94% in 2007-12 ([Bhalla 2016](https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.116.014136)), and Tartu from 34% to 88% in 2001-03, with its 1991-93 value given as 72% in one source and 82% in another ([Kõrv 1999](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0404.1999.tb07340.x)). Oxfordshire is the exception that defines the trap. There 54.6% of community-onset first strokes were admitted within a month in the 1980s and 63.0% of incident strokes were hospitalised in 2002-17 ([Bamford 1986](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.292.6532.1369); [Li 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/2396987319881017)), because a same-day clinic absorbed the minor strokes that elsewhere came to be admitted. The figure is not an artefact of case-finding, since a direct check of primary-care records for 90,542 people found only 2 additional incident strokes ([Coull 2004](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.STR.0000137605.48864.2f)). Since 2000 the registers therefore split by service design and not by access: **88% to 99% where care is organised around admission, and 54% to 71% where minor strokes are deliberately managed in rapid-access clinics, emergency departments or general practice**. No percentage could be sourced for Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Israel or the Gulf states. The classic US national estimate did not observe the quantity at all: it assumed that 10% of strokes were not hospitalised, a value chosen between two population studies reporting 5% and 15% ([Williams 2001](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2377-1-2)).

Around 2000 the evidence for Central and Eastern Europe and the former Soviet Union splits in two. Urban registers with strong hospital pathways report 88% (Tartu) and 89.7% (Grodno), while a third to a half or more stayed at home in Uzhgorod (66% admitted), Varna (46% at ages 45 to 84) and Krasnodar (39.7%) ([Kulesh 2010](https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.110.596916); [Mihálka 2001](https://doi.org/10.1161/hs1001.097107); [Powles 2002](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2458-2-24); [Kharakoz 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12830526/)). Nothing population-based was retrieved for the region after 2003. MONICA adds 22 population-periods for 1982 to 1995, but only at ages 25 to 64 or 74, and its Data Centre warns that the share managed outside hospital "is more dependent on the organization of medical care and perhaps on the definition of the term 'hospital' than on the coverage of registration" ([MONICA quality report](https://www.thl.fi/publications/monica/strokeqa/strokeqa.htm)). At ages 65 to 74 the in-hospital share of non-fatal events was 56% to 59% in Novosibirsk, and in the Novosibirsk control area at ages 25 to 64 it fell from 91% in 1987 to 67% in 1993 ([MONICA quality report, Table 11](https://www.thl.fi/publications/monica/strokeqa/table11.htm)). The notes read that fall as economic disruption, and it shows that the trend is not monotone. The notes found no review that pools the percentage hospitalised or reports it by income level, so their tables are the pooled evidence base as far as anyone has assembled one.

Who is missed differs in kind between settings. In Porto 65.1% of the non-hospitalised had an NIHSS score under 5 ([Abreu 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2021.685821)), so in rich systems the non-admitted are mostly the mild, the very old in care homes and those dead before arrival. In Uzhgorod the hospitalised were more than 10 years younger than those treated at home, with 30-day case fatality of 15.4% against 36.8% ([Mihálka 2001](https://doi.org/10.1161/hs1001.097107)), so where access was the constraint the old, the rural and the severe stayed home.

### China and India: a township, a 40-case sample and three studies from the mid-2000s

China has one within-population series, and it is one township. In Yangjinzhuang, a low-income rural township of about 15,000 people in Tianjin, the share of first-ever strokes admitted to a county or tertiary hospital rose from **6.9% in 1992-2000 to 16.1% in 2001-09 and 54.2% in 2010-18** ([Han 2021](https://doi.org/10.2147/RMHP.S325822)). The slope of an interrupted time series rose from 0.011 to 0.044 a year at the 2009 insurance reform, and among those aged 65 and over the share was 6.3% before the reform and 42.9% after ([Liu 2021](https://doi.org/10.7189/jogh.11.08002)). The authors say the township "is not representative of the overall rural population of China". The only other direct value is from Sichuan, where 70.0% (28 of 40) of incident first-ever strokes in 2012-13, fatal and non-fatal, were hospitalised within 7 days ([Front Neurol 2020](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2020.00877)). The national survey of which Sichuan is a part documented CT or MRI in 83% of 1,643 incident strokes ([Wang 2017](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.116.025250)), which measures contact and not admission. Beijing's MONICA register saw the in-hospital share of non-fatal events at ages 25 to 64 rise from 81% in 1984 to 92% in 1993 ([MONICA quality report, Table 11](https://www.thl.fi/publications/monica/strokeqa/table11.htm)). For Southeast Asia and Oceania nothing was verified, and the Nha Trang incidence study in Vietnam computes incidence on "the assumption that all FES cases in Nha Trang city were admitted" ([Yamanashi 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4981455/)).

India's direct evidence is three studies, two of them known here only through a systematic review. In Mumbai in 2005-06, 67.2% (306 of 456) of first-ever strokes were admitted to health-care facilities and the other 32.8% "either died outside of hospital or were treated at home or nursing homes" ([Dalal 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19086353/)). The review gives 69% for Kolkata in 2003-05 and 26% for twelve villages in rural West Bengal ([Jones 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/17474930211027834)). Nothing comparable has been verified since. The national registry programme of 2018-19 finds its cases through hospitals, imaging centres and death registration, so it reports a 7.1% share of death-certificate-only cases (derived in the notes) but no admitted share ([Rangamani 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lansea.2023.100308)). A 2024-25 survey of survivors in tea-estate communities in Assam found 60.4% admitted, which is an upper bound for all incident strokes because the dead are missing from it ([Nambiar 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13504897/)).

### Latin America is measured, but sub-Saharan Africa and North Africa and the Middle East are not

Latin America has more direct measurements than any other developing region in the notes. In Iquique, Chile, in 2000-02 the hospital admission rate was 71% (207 of 292) for all first-ever strokes and 82% (151 of 185) for first-ever ischaemic stroke, two figures with different denominators that are not in conflict ([Lavados 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15978929/); [Lavados 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17239801/)). In Ñuble, Chile, in 2015-16, 85.7% of 890 first-ever strokes were admitted and 13.6% were known only from the death registry ([Lavados 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X%2820%2930470-8)). In Joinville, Brazil, in 2005-06, 84.2% of 759 were diagnosed in emergency rooms ([Cabral 2009](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp.2008.164475)), and Matão, Brazil, is cited at 97% by another paper ([Cantu-Brito 2010](https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.109.577726)). The notes read a central value near 85%.

For sub-Saharan Africa the notes found no study that reports the share of all incident strokes admitted, and what exists are bounds. The Tanzanian Stroke Incidence Project identified 636 strokes in 2003-06, of which its surveillance system, in hospital or in the community, saw 201 alive. That is 29% in rural Hai and 38% in urban Dar-es-Salaam by the notes' arithmetic, the remainder being known only from verbal autopsy ([Walker 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422%2810%2970144-7); [Walker 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X%2813%2970068-8)). In Freetown, among stroke deaths under age 70 found only by verbal autopsy, 48.5% had sought formal health care and 19.0% died in hospital ([Youkee 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/17474930251367517)). The Lagos registry's 84.6% has facility-presenting cases as its denominator ([Danesi 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jns.2013.04.026)), and surveys of intention in Tanzania and Uganda find 88.3% and 85.2% saying they would go to hospital ([Hertz 2019](https://doi.org/10.1159/000499069); [Nakibuuka 2014](https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/309106)). A figure that circulates in the Nigerian literature, that "close to one third of stroke patients may not get to hospital", is referenced to a 1997 comparison of registries in Dijon ([Sanya 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4441143/)). None of these is the target. The notes' reading, offered as judgment, is an admitted share of plausibly 0.2 to 0.5.

For North Africa and the Middle East nothing was verified. The Mashhad incidence study of 2006-07 states that "A large number of volunteers assisted in finding stroke patients not admitted to hospital", but its abstract gives no proportion ([Azarpazhooh 2010](https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.109.559708)). The Isfahan study says of itself that "it did not cover patients with stroke who were not hospitalized" ([Oveisgharan 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4852074/)), and the open door-to-door prevalence papers from Egypt, Libya, Tunisia, Lebanon and Morocco that were read say nothing on acute care. The GBD count table nevertheless lists nine literature incidence sources for the region ([GBD 2019 stroke paper appendix](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422%2821%2900252-0)), so there are full texts to check.

### Which gaps are paywalls and which are absences

The publisher sites for *Stroke*, the *Lancet* family and Karger journals refused automated retrieval, so several sources were read only as abstracts or secondary citations, and someone with library access could close these gaps. For China they are the national survey ([Wang 2017](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.116.025250)), Sino-MONICA Beijing ([Zhao 2008](https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.107.502807)) and the three-city study ([Jiang 2006](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.STR.0000194955.34820.78)), and for India the Kolkata and rural West Bengal primaries ([Das 2007](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.STR.0000258111.00319.58); [Bhattacharya 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16821657/)). For sub-Saharan Africa the one to read is the Tanzanian main paper, for the share of surveillance-identified cases admitted and the place of death of the verbal-autopsy cases ([Walker 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422%2810%2970144-7)), and for North Africa and the Middle East it is Mashhad ([Azarpazhooh 2010](https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.109.559708)). Ulaanbaatar ([Banzrai 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X%2823%2900130-4)) and the Matão primary paper ([Minelli 2007](https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.107.484139)) would add two more values. For Europe and the high-income world about 50 named incidence studies remain, together with an Italian review whose abstract reports "a hospitalization rate from 82% to 98%" across registers and which probably tabulates it for each ([Sacco 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1747-4949.2011.00594.x)). The absences are of a different kind: for Pakistan, Nepal, Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, Ghana, Kenya and Ethiopia, among others, the notes found no data at all on the share of strokes hospitalised.

## Five other strategies, each with its strength beside its bias

The notes price none of this in person-time, so the statements about cost below are my ordinal judgments from the tasks each strategy requires.

### Pool the population-based studies in a logit meta-regression

This strategy rests on the one design that observes the target directly, the prospective population-based incidence study with multiple overlapping sources that looks for cases outside hospital. The evidence today is the set of tables in the notes: 52 study-periods and 22 MONICA population-periods for high-income countries, Europe and the former Soviet Union, and about a dozen direct values for low- and middle-income countries, three of them secondary citations. Its strength is that it measures the admitted share itself and includes series under a constant protocol in Auckland, South London, Tartu and Tianjin. Its weakness is that the sites are cities and districts with research infrastructure, that numerators and denominators differ in every way the trap table lists, and that the regression must extrapolate to sub-Saharan Africa and to North Africa and the Middle East from no local observation at all. Countries without data and years from 1990 to 2023 are covered by prediction from a covariate, and years to 2050 only if the covariate can itself be forecast, which the end of this section takes up. The functional form has a precedent inside GBD in the prehospital myocardial infarction scalar, and the notes judge a regression on calendar year, age window and a system-type indicator feasible with about 50 to 70 points for the high-income and European set alone. The cost is low to moderate: the seed tables exist, roughly 75 named full texts remain to be read (my count of the notes' could-not-verify lists), and the rest is coding and fitting.

### Divide administrative admissions by expected incidence, or link hospital and death registers

This strategy rests on routine discharge counts over GBD incidence, or on linked registers that define an incident stroke as a first admission or a death without one. The multi-country series are the OECD's (46 countries, 1980 to 2025), Eurostat's (36 countries) and the WHO European database (1970 to 2023). All of them count discharges for the broad I60-I69 group and not persons with acute stroke, and the latest OECD rates run from 1.0 per 100,000 in Colombia to 823.3 in Bulgaria ([OECD hospital discharges](https://sdmx.oecd.org/public/rest/data/OECD.ELS.HD,DSD_HEALTH_PROC@DF_HOSP_DISCHARGE,/.....DICDA908............?dimensionAtObservation=AllDimensions); [WHO European Health for All database](https://gateway.euro.who.int/en/indicators/hfa_398-2480-hospital-discharges-cerebrovascular-diseases-per-100-000)). When the notes set these beside GBD 2023 first-ever incidence for 2019, the discharge rate is the larger of the two in every country with a complete national discharge system: 344.2 discharges against 187.6 incident strokes per 100,000 in Poland, 259.4 against 141.3 in France and 160.7 against 114.3 in Chile, for example. It is the smaller only where reporting is partial, as in Colombia (2.0 against 89.1) and Mexico (41.6 against 77.5); the notes tabulate all 46 countries. A would-be fraction that exceeds 1 in every complete health system is measuring coding breadth, transfers, readmissions and recurrent events, not access. The comparison is also partly circular, since GBD incidence in these countries is itself fitted to the same inpatient data after division by the crosswalk factor. Scotland shows the dependence from the other side: Public Health Scotland counts 9,281 incident strokes in 2019/20 by first admission or death, against 16,534 in GBD 2023 for 2019 ([Scottish Stroke Statistics](https://publichealthscotland.scot/media/31155/2025-01-28-stroke-report_final.pdf); [IHME GBD 2023 results tool](https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results/)).

The linked-register version has a real strength, person-level first-ever counts: in Norway in 2001-21, **92.4% of 229,857 register-defined incident strokes were inpatient hospitalisations and 7.6% were out-of-hospital deaths** ([Kvalvaag 2025](https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.125.041029)). Its weakness sits in the definition, because a non-fatal stroke that is never admitted appears in neither register, and 12% of first-ever strokes in a Swedish community register were missing from both ([Appelros 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0404.2010.01402.x)). The method therefore bounds the admitted share from above, at about 90% to 96% in the best-served systems. Coverage is about 46 mostly high-income countries, mostly from 2000 on, with nothing comparable elsewhere and no forecast, and the notes found no published analysis that divides national admissions by expected incident strokes for any low- or middle-income country. The cost is low for the downloads, which the notes ran through public interfaces, and moderate for the one extension that would add information. That extension is to ask the Scottish and Swedish agencies for the split between hospitalised cases and deaths without admission, which they hold and do not publish, and to watch whether the European Stroke Service Tracker releases the "coverage" it now computes from national incident totals and registry admissions ([Stroke Service Tracker guide](https://actionplan.eso-stroke.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/SAP-E-Stroke-Services-Tracker-Guide-for-NCs_2024.pdf)).

### Read reach from where and how stroke deaths occur

This strategy rests on verbal autopsy, sample registration and vital registration, which record where stroke deaths occur (call the in-facility share H) and sometimes whether hospital care was sought in the final illness (C). Its strength is reach: it is the only family of sources with national samples in India, China and several African countries, and with some series over time. The stroke-specific numbers published so far are few. In rural Gadchiroli in 2011-13, 87.3% of stroke deaths occurred at home ([Kalkonde 2015](https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.115.008918)), and in Faridabad in 2019-20, at ages 30 to 69, 38.4% died in hospital while 56.2% had visited one (derived in the notes) ([Krishnan 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lansea.2023.100222)). In northern Vietnam in 2008, 57% of verbal-autopsy stroke deaths had been admitted at some point in the illness ([J Neurosci Rural Pract 2012](https://doi.org/10.4103/0976-3147.102603)). Across China's national mortality surveillance for 2008-20, **17.6% of 3.2 million stroke deaths occurred in medical institutions and 78.7% at home**, with no trend ([Lancet Reg Health West Pac 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanwpc.2022.100383)).

The weakness is that H is dominated by where frail and dying people are cared for. In the China Kadoorie Biobank 35.7% of decedents hospitalised in their last week were discharged to die at home, 66.0% in the rural and urban residents' insurance scheme against 11.6% in the employee scheme ([Weng 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanwpc.2021.100301)). In the United States and Sweden under half of stroke deaths occur in hospital (47.0% in each) because 35.4% and 43.9% occur in nursing homes ([CDC 2006](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm5519a2.htm); [Int J Cardiol Cardiovasc Risk Prev 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijcrp.2024.200328)), while Brazil records 78.0% in hospital ([de Souza 2025](https://doi.org/10.5334/gh.1476)). H is therefore a floor for C and unusable as a level across regions. It is more defensible for gradients and trends within a region, and even there the flat Chinese series is a warning. Whether fatal strokes are admitted more or less often than non-fatal ones is not settled by anything in the notes. Countries without data could borrow the structure of an existing all-cause model of home deaths, built on 152 country-years in 49 countries with urbanisation, home births, health spending, income and education, and region as predictors ([Adair 2021](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgh-2021-006766)), and nothing forecasts the quantity. The cost is the highest of the five, since the notes describe a harmonised re-analysis as a data-access project and not a literature extraction. Place of death by cause sits in every WHO-instrument verbal autopsy dataset, and in a Million Death Study sample of roughly 41,000 stroke deaths that is untabulated by place ([Ke 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X%2818%2930242-0)), while the care-seeking items that would give C were dropped from the WHO instrument in its 2022 revision ([WHO verbal autopsy repository](https://github.com/SwissTPH/WHO-VA)).

### Ask survivors, or count survey-reported admissions

This strategy rests on household and ageing surveys, either the share of survivors who report having been hospitalised (S) or the rate of survey-reported stroke admissions divided by modelled incidence. Its strength is a population denominator and, in India, public microdata. The one survey that asked incident and prevalent cases the same question shows the bias directly: in Sichuan 90.1% of prevalent survivors but 70.0% of incident strokes had been hospitalised within 7 days ([Front Neurol 2020](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2020.00877)). S overstated the target by 20 points there, and the notes treat S as an upper bound wherever early case fatality is high. The large ageing surveys are less useful than their size suggests. LASI and SAGE identify stroke by a health professional's diagnosis, which already conditions on contact with the system, and neither asks whether the respondent was hospitalised for the stroke, though both record stroke as a coded reason for the last hospitalisation ([LASI questionnaire](https://iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/LASI_W1_Individual_Questionnaire.pdf); [SAGE questionnaire](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/SAGE-India-Wave1-Individual_Q.pdf)). India's National Sample Survey of 2017-18 gives 46 stroke hospitalisations per 100,000 persons ([Rajasulochana and Kar 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/14737167.2021.1941883)). Against GBD 2023 incidence of 110.3 per 100,000 for India in 2017 ([IHME GBD 2023 results tool](https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results/)) that is a ratio of about **0.42**, a calculation in the notes and not a published estimate. It counts recurrent strokes and readmissions over a first-ever denominator and misses admissions in households that dissolved, and it is still the only national, population-denominated number for India that the notes found. Coverage is the survey countries and years only, with no forecast. The cost is moderate: analysis of public files with small cells, plus one paywalled paper or an approach to its authors for the national Chinese figure.

### Assume a rule in a covariate

The last strategy measures nothing: it assumes that the admitted share is a stated function of an index. GBD does this for the share of injuries treated (the HAQ rule quoted above), and the one global analysis with an explicit utilisation term for stroke did the same with a survey proxy. Kruk and colleagues assigned ischaemic stroke and intracerebral haemorrhage a visit rate of **88% in low-income, 88% in lower-middle-income and 92% in upper-middle-income countries**, taken from a 2002-03 World Health Survey item on care sought "at in- or outpatient facility" by people who needed care "for asthma, heart disease or minor surgery" ([Kruk 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2818%2931668-4)). The strength is complete coverage at almost no cost and an assumption stated in the open. The weakness is that it is an assumption, and Kruk's version would mean nearly nine in ten strokes admitted in low-income countries, far above every direct rural observation in the notes. I would use a rule only for what the data cannot supply, which is a stated floor.

### Covariates decide the coverage of missing countries and of 2050

Every strategy reaches countries without data, and years to 2050, only through a covariate, so the choice of covariate matters as much as the choice of strategy. The HAQ Index is the obvious candidate. It is served as a GBD 2023 covariate for 1980 to 2023 and 934 locations with uncertainty bounds, ranging in 2023 from 88.9 in Japan through 70.3 in China and 41.7 in India (19.9 in 1990) to 16.1 in Somalia ([IHME GBD 2023 covariates](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2825%2901637-X)). Its own authors suggested that "the 0–100 scale approximates 0–100%" coverage ([HAQ Index 2016 paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2818%2930994-2)), and GBD already uses it this way for injuries and myocardial infarction. Its weaknesses sit close to the use proposed here. Stroke is one of its 32 causes, entering as a risk-standardised death rate below age 75, so nothing above age 74 informs it ([HAQ Index 2019 paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X%2822%2900429-6)). Stroke incidence in data-poor countries is itself produced by a model in which excess mortality depends on HAQ, which makes the index circular for this purpose. It also blends access with quality, which the third and fourth factors claim as well, and the notes found no published forecast of it. The UHC effective coverage stroke indicator is worse for this purpose, since it is the stroke mortality-to-incidence ratio ([Lozano 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2930750-9)) and so moves with stroke-unit quality as much as with reaching hospital, which risks counting the same improvement twice. The all-cause inpatient utilisation envelope is the closest in kind to the target, at 0.02 admissions per person in Cambodia against 0.27 in Bulgaria in 2016 ([Moses 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667%2818%2930213-5)), but it is a model output driven by spending, beds and SDI where data are absent, and no public file of the GBD 2023 version was found.

For the 2050 horizon the notes confirmed published forecasts only for health spending ([Lancet 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2821%2901258-7)) and, from a download page seen only as a search summary, for urban share ([UN World Urbanization Prospects](https://population.un.org/wup/downloads)), while SDI is forecast inside IHME's own forecasting work ([GBD 2021 forecasting paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900685-8)). A model that must be projected is therefore safer specified on SDI or income, health spending per capita and urban share, or else on HAQ with an explicit and crude HAQ-on-SDI bridge. Two cautions apply to any of these. The GBD forecast of incident strokes is forecast deaths divided by an SDI-driven mortality-to-incidence ratio, so a second factor driven by SDI is strongly correlated with the first factor's own driver. And a national covariate value attached to a city-based study understates that site's access, which biases national predictions upward at low covariate values. This is my inference from the urban-site trap, and the remedy is to use subnational values where they exist. The notes found no published study that regresses the hospitalised share of incident strokes on income or health-system variables. A first-look regression of the study-level admitted share on the HAQ Index is being run separately and will be reported alongside this report, and nothing here anticipates its results.

## The notes support a bracketed meta-regression, starting with China and India

No single strategy answers the question, and the notes agree on how the pieces fit together. The core is the meta-regression of population-based studies, because it is the only strategy that observes the target, fitted in logit space on a development or access covariate with an age term, a calendar-time term and the design covariates of the trap table. The top of the curve is anchored twice, by the GBD crosswalk (about 0.80 to 0.95 of incident strokes captured by hospital-only counts in systems like those behind the matched pairs) and by linked registers (at most about 90% to 96% admitted in the best-served systems). The countries that matter most are bracketed. The notes' assessment is that a strategy using the in-hospital share of stroke deaths as a lower anchor and the survivor-reported share as an upper anchor is defensible, and that using either alone is not. China is the worked example, with 17.6% of stroke deaths in institutions, 70.0% of incident strokes hospitalised in a 40-case sample and 90.1% of survivors. The bottom of the curve needs a stated floor, in the way GBD states its injury rule, because no administrative source can supply one. Administrative ratios contribute nothing below the top of the range.

I read the proposed regression as the GBD hospital-only crosswalk with a location covariate added. A population-based study that reports its admitted share is a within-study matched pair, and the log of that share is, to the approximation that annual incidence is small, the logit difference that the crosswalk estimates. That reading is mine and not the notes', and it has two uses. It gives a consistency check, since the fitted curve evaluated for the systems and eras behind the GBD pairs should return something near 0.8 to 0.95. It also says what a successful result would imply for GBD itself, namely that hospital-only sources in low-access settings are currently adjusted by a factor learned in high-access ones.

For orientation, the notes read their tables as the working ranges below, which are judgment and not findings.

| Setting | Working range for the admitted share (the notes' judgment) |
|---|---|
| Admission-centred high-income systems since 2000 | 0.91 to 0.99 |
| High-income systems that manage minor strokes in clinics | 0.54 to 0.71 admitted, with near-complete hospital contact |
| Central and Eastern Europe and the former Soviet Union around 2000 (nothing since 2003) | 0.40 to 0.90, urban registers at the top |
| Upper-middle-income Latin America since 2000 | 0.70 to 0.95 |
| Urban India in the mid-2000s (no verified recent value) | 0.65 to 0.70 |
| Rural India | 0.25 to 0.6 |
| Rural China | Under 0.1 in the 1990s, rising to 0.5 to 0.6 by the late 2010s |
| Sub-Saharan Africa (no direct observation) | 0.2 to 0.5, with wide uncertainty |
| North Africa and the Middle East | No basis for a range |

For 1990 to 2050 the evidence gives direction but not pace. Within-site series rose by 36 points over 40 years in Auckland and by 12 points over 15 years in South London. Tianjin rose by about 1.1 points a year before its insurance reform and 4.4 after (the notes' conversion of the published slopes), and the Novosibirsk MONICA register fell in the early 1990s. The time path therefore depends on financing and service design more than on the calendar, and a projection should carry scenarios and not a single trend.

What to do first follows from where the incident strokes are and from what is cheap, and the ordering is my reading of the notes. The first step costs nothing: write the definition down (admitted as an inpatient within about 7 days, ischaemic and intracerebral only, first-ever as GBD counts them, with a rule for emergency-department-only, in-hospital-onset and late-rehabilitation cases), because every extraction decision depends on it. The second is the IHME pull, with two items ahead of the rest: the matched pairs behind the `cv_hospital` crosswalk, and the excluded inpatient stroke data for India and China, the two countries that dominate the calculation. The third is library work, again China and India first. The national Chinese survey paper comes before anything else, since its Sichuan sub-study reports hospitalisation within 7 days and implies that the item was collected for the national sample of 1,643 incident strokes. After it come the Kolkata and rural West Bengal primaries, Sino-MONICA Beijing and the three-city study, and then the Tanzanian main paper and Mashhad, which are the only identified prospects of a direct value for sub-Saharan Africa and for North Africa and the Middle East. The fourth is to code the study table with the design covariates and fit the model, of which the first-look HAQ regression reported alongside this report is the start. Only after that would I decide whether the expensive data-access work (verbal autopsy microdata, the public NSS and LASI files, an approach to the Chinese survey's authors or to China CDC, requests to the Scottish and Swedish agencies) is worth its cost, on the basis of how wide the intervals for China and India remain.

## Conclusion

The second factor turns out to be two different problems. In high-income systems it is close to saturation, and what remains is definitional: whether a minor stroke imaged in a clinic and sent home counts, and whether a death certified as stroke before arrival belongs in the denominator. In China, India and sub-Saharan Africa it is an empirical unknown. Its plausible range (0.2 to 0.5 for sub-Saharan Africa, 0.25 to 0.7 across rural and urban India) is wide beside the published uncertainty interval on the incident count it multiplies, so it deserves an interval of its own in the final estimate and not a point value. That is also where a stroke-unit scale-up runs out of reach, because raising stroke-unit coverage among the admitted does nothing for people whose pathway ends at a village doctor or a neighbourhood practitioner ([Liu 2021](https://doi.org/10.7189/jogh.11.08002); [Krishnan 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lansea.2023.100222)). A scenario that holds the second factor fixed caps the share of incident strokes that can benefit at the admitted share, however many stroke units are built.

The four factors are also less independent than the multiplication suggests. SDI drives both the incidence forecast and any covariate model of reach, inpatient records feed both GBD incidence and any admissions-based estimate of reach, and reach and stroke-unit coverage share a denominator that audits and registers define differently. I would therefore simulate the product jointly, with the two brackets carried through, and not multiply four point estimates. An explicit second factor is more than the precedents offer. WHO-CHOICE folds reach into a fixed coverage of "hospitalized patients with stroke". It arrives at 43 to 54 DALYs averted per million population per year in its 2012 version and 1.4 to 1.8 healthy life years per million per year in 2022 ([Ortegon 2012](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.e607); [WHO 2022 technical annex](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/ncds/mnd/2022-app3-technical-annex-v26jan2023.pdf)), and any estimate built on the Cochrane odds ratio should expect to be compared with those figures. The supply of evidence is not improving on its own, and what would settle the question is what the World Stroke Organization and Lancet Neurology Commission has already asked governments for, "national stroke registries of hospitalised, non-hospitalised, fatal, and non-fatal strokes" ([Feigin 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422%2823%2900277-6)).
