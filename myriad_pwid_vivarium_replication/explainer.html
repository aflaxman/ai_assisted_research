Replication note

Re-running a microsimulation of US people who inject drugs, from its published inputs

Chiosi and colleagues project 487,000 injection-related deaths and $565 billion in healthcare costs among US people who inject drugs over 2025–2034. Their model, MYRIAD, is not public. This page explains how the model was rebuilt in vivarium from the paper's tables, where it agrees with the published numbers, where it does not, and how its inputs sit against the Global Burden of Disease estimates.

What the paper does

MYRIAD follows a closed cohort of 3.7 million people with past-year injection drug use, one month at a time. Each person carries an age, a sex, a drug-use state, an HIV state, an HCV state and a flag for an uncured bacterial infection. Every month the model draws transitions from published probabilities, draws death from the sum of background and cause-specific hazards, and picks the cause in proportion to those hazards. Costs attach to states and events and are discounted at 3% a year to 2025.

The cohort is the 2018 estimate of Bradley et al.; the model ages it through 2024 so that about 3.3 million remain at the start of 2025, then reports 2025–2034. Two scenarios move the overdose-death trend: a Sustained Decline extrapolating the 2021–2024 fall, and a Precipitous Increase extrapolating the pandemic-era rise.

Drug useLow, medium, high (OUD) risk and remission; five calibrated monthly transition probabilities; overdose death by state and calendar year.
HIVInfection, diagnosis by testing, ART start at diagnosis and monthly start/stop, CD4 progression by regimen, HIV death by CD4 and ART.
HCVInfection and reinfection, spontaneous clearance, METAVIR F0–F4, decompensated cirrhosis, hepatocellular carcinoma; no treatment.
Severe bacterial infectionHospitalisation for SSTI, endocarditis, osteomyelitis or CNS abscess; in-hospital death by type; cure unless self-directed discharge.
MortalityCause-deleted life table plus the four cause-specific hazards; rates summed, cause drawn in proportion.
CostsMonthly state costs for drug use, HIV (CD4 and ART) and HCV stage; per-event costs for hospitalisations and overdose deaths.

Why vivarium, not camdl

vivarium chosen

IHME's microsimulation framework. One component per condition owns its state columns and updates them each step; a value pipeline pools the cause-specific death probabilities; an observer tallies events and costs by year. Each of the paper's input tables becomes a few lines of a component, and the per-person bookkeeping (age, costs, cause of death) is native.

camdl not used

A compiler and inference stack for stochastic compartmental models fitted to time series. MYRIAD could be flattened into compartments, but the product state space runs to tens of thousands of strata, camdl has no notion of per-person costs or discounting, and there is nothing to infer: the paper's only calibrated inputs are published as point values.

Does it replicate?

Running the paper's inputs (Table 1 and 31 supplement tables, every one cited in the code) through the re-implementation reproduces the epidemiology. Three seeds of 369,450 simulants stand in for the 3.7 million PWID; the paper's own simulation SD is about 29,000 deaths and 36,000 infections.

Two readings of the methods were needed

The paper's text admits two interpretations on each of two points, and only one of each reproduces its numbers. Both are flags in the code with the alternative kept as a sensitivity run.

  • People in remission keep the injecting infection risks. The supplement defines remission as "not engaging in active IDU" and provides separate non-IDU inputs, but treating the 16% of the cohort in remission as non-injecting leaves infections 13–23% short of the paper's annual counts.
  • An uncured infection keeps its mortality until cure. The 2023 model description says infection type affects mortality "only in the month the infection occurs", which gives a 1.75% case fatality per hospitalisation; the paper's own counts imply 2.2%. Letting the 19% of OUD patients who self-discharge stay exposed until cured closes most of the gap.

What does not replicate

The cost split. Drug-use costs come out 25% high and HIV costs 17% high, HCV costs 16% low; severe-infection costs match. The drug-use state mix cannot be the cause, because overdose deaths depend on the same mix and match to 1%. The paper's 2025 drug-use cost works out to $1,370 per person-month, while its inputs (83% of the cohort in the OUD state at $1,791 plus a $115 syringe cost, 16% in remission at $765) give $1,715. Neither dropping the syringe cost nor discounting from 2018 instead of 2025 is stated, and the severe-infection cost per hospitalisation ($16,000 implied by the paper against $16,200 from its Table 1) rules out a hidden discount factor. The HIV gap points the same way as the HIV death shortfall: the replication keeps more people on ART than the paper does, though the ART inputs are reproduced as printed.

Two summary statistics in the paper cannot be derived from its own numbers. "6.4 life-years per person" is impossible for a 3.25 million cohort that loses 667,000 people in 10 years, which gives about 9; and the "$17,375 average annual cost per person" equals total cost divided by 3.254 million times ten years, which is not a per-person-year figure.

Against the Global Burden of Disease

GBD 2023 estimates cover the whole US population; MYRIAD covers 3.7 million active injectors. Most rows therefore compare a subset with a total, and the interesting question is whether the subset is a plausible share.

  • Overdose deaths are consistent. The cohort's 48,800 overdose deaths in 2023 are 51% of GBD's 95,300 drug-use-disorder deaths, close to the 45% injection share the paper applies to national counts.
  • Opioid use disorder is a higher-risk subset. The 2.8 million in the model's OUD state are 40% of GBD's 7.0 million prevalent cases; their overdose death probability, 1.66% a year, is above the 1.04% implied by GBD's deaths and prevalence.
  • The number of PWID is the largest input disagreement. Bradley et al.'s 3.7 million exceeds the 2.6 million implied by GBD's injected-drug-use exposure (1.06% of males, 0.48% of females in 2022). The paper's sensitivity range, 1.9 to 7.3 million, spans both.
  • HIV: more HIV among PWID than GBD attributes to drug use. GBD puts 6.6% of US HIV deaths (376) on drug use; the model's PWID have 1,090. The paper's introduction cites "nearly 100,000 PWID living with HIV", half its own 5.12% input.
  • HCV burden sits mostly outside the active cohort. GBD attributes 89% of 38,000 US HCV deaths to lifetime injection, but the model's current injectors account for 5,800; the rest fall among older former injectors a closed cohort cannot hold.
  • Endocarditis is in range at about a tenth of GBD's 67,700 cases, and the supplement's cause-deleted life table matches GBD's all-cause q(x) within 2 per 1,000 from age 50 while sitting below it at 25–49, where the removed drug, HIV and HCV deaths concentrate.

Run it

cd myriad_pwid_vivarium_replication
uv sync
uv run pytest -q                                      # 8 tests, ~10 s
uv run python run_replication.py --n 100000 --seeds 0 --scenarios base   # ~4 min
uv run python plot_results.py
uv run python gbd_comparison.py

The inputs live in myriad/parameters.py with a citation on every value, the state machines in myriad/components.py, and the comparison tables and figures under results/. The full runs behind the figures above used 369,450 simulants per replicate and three seeds for the base case.
