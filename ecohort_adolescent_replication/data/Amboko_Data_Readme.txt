DATASET ------------ Title: MNH eCohort datasetDescription: The dataset used in this analysis comes from the Maternal and Newborn Health (MNH) eCohort study conducted in Ethiopia, Kenya, and South Africa. It is a longitudinal dataset designed to assess the quality, continuity, and experiences of maternal healthcare across the care continuum. Women were enrolled face-to-face during their first antenatal care (ANC) visit at selected public and private health facilities and followed through phone interviews during pregnancy, childbirth, and the postnatal period. The cohort includes pregnant women aged 15 years and older who planned to continue care in the same area and had not yet received ANC from a skilled provider. The primary exposure variable is age group, comparing adolescents (15–19 years) to adult women (20 years and above). Data were collected using structured interviews at enrolment and during follow-up, capturing clinical care, health system interactions, and maternal outcomes. The dataset offers rich information for examining adolescent versus adult maternal care experiences and outcomes in low-resource settings, and can support research on health system performance, equity, and maternal health policy.Publication Year: 2026Creator(s): Beatrice AmbokoOrganisation(s):  KEMRI-Wellcome Trust Research ProgrammeSource(s): NA3. METHOD and PROCESSING --------------------------  Data for the MNH eCohort were collected using structured questionnaires administered face-to-face at enrolment and via phone interviews during follow-up periods. Data collection tools were programmed using SurveyCTO software (Dobility, version current at time of data collection), which enabled real-time data capture on Android-based tablets during in-person interviews and secure remote entry during phone surveys. Standardised survey instruments were developed collaboratively by the study team and piloted for contextual relevance in Ethiopia, Kenya, and South Africa. These instruments included validated items on maternal care quality, respectful care, complications, and health system interactions. Data were cleaned, recoded, and processed in Stata (version 17), including checks for consistency and handling of missing values. The dataset used in this analysis was restricted to women with complete follow-up through six weeks postpartum.6. RELATED DATASET(S)-------------------NA7.
